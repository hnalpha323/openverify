# Watchlist matching: the Workflow 6C calibration (C78.4, C78.7, C78.8)

This report decides, with measurements, the five matching decisions the owner took after Workflow 6B (`docs/plans/phase-6.plan.md`, "Owner decisions after Workflow 6B", decisions 1 to 5). It measures the matcher of the watchlist reports at Workflow 6C's base and at the head of 6C's Stage 1, on one evaluation seed, and gives a verdict for each decision and for each constant or rule that Stage 1 changed: kept, or reverted with the commits that make the change.

Every number here comes from synthetic data. **Synthetic variation is not real-world variation**: the typos, orders, name frequencies and customer populations below are generated by rules, and real lists, real customers and real data entry differ from them in ways no generator captures. The numbers describe the matcher's behaviour on these sets. They are not an accuracy claim about real screening.

The calibration changes no code, constant, threshold, preset, cap, build script, benchmark script or generator. It runs them. A benchmark step that cannot run because of a defect is fixed test-first in `packages/watchlist/scripts/benchmark-*` only, never changing what a metric means, and listed under "Amendments". Every other finding is for its owner.

## Method

This section and the decision rules below were written and committed before the evaluation seed was drawn. A change made afterwards is listed under "Amendments", with its reason.

### What is compared

- **Base:** Workflow 6C's base, `94ab63f92a0f527d723c313f0087bb7ba24d4406`, with the head's `packages/watchlist/scripts/benchmark-*` and `packages/watchlist/src/testing/held-out/` overlaid, uncommitted, so both trees run the same benchmark and the same generators.
- **Head:** the head of Workflow 6C's Stage 1, `e880a9fcc8e3ebe721f89601fa6ac931b6c5c54d`. It holds every Stage 1 commit of `matcher-6c`, `evaluation-set-6c`, `ingestion-6c` and `api-reports-6c`.
- **Two worktrees** pinned to those commits (`/tmp/cal-base`, `/tmp/cal-head`), never the main tree, which another agent edits during Stage 2. Each is prepared with `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile --offline` and the `data/` files copied from the main tree, except `variant-groups.json`. Each builds its own shipped variant groups with its own script: base SHA-256 `529216c9da43b57323b66b2b8ae98e7997e1b5972be34457f6042b0eb6f27324`, head `29d0583854563f81084c05d78b5ea8805ca14630cbf655349cfa09fca84c3bfb` (the head's file adds the MIT notice, decision 19). The group source is `data/rigour-person_names.txt`, SHA-256 `be47a435969da3fa7311d8c682e462d6d20e6cf27cf90ad1473ce213c9a91c79`, as `data/manifest.json` pins it.
- **One seed** for both trees. Base and head screen the same list, cases, customers and tables, so every difference is paired.

### Independence (H21, V12)

- **The seed** is drawn after this method is committed, with a CSPRNG (`openssl rand -hex 16`), long after the matcher's last Stage 1 commit. It is recorded under "Seed and data". Nobody knew the held-out groups or the populations before it existed.
- **The hold-out build.** Each tree runs its own `build:variant-groups --hold-out <seed> --output <dir outside git>`, which drops the tenth of the groups that `isHeldOutGroup(id, seed)` selects. The 6B set is screened against that build only, and the benchmark asserts before any screening that no group a held-out case draws on survives in it.
- **The scale sets** hold no held-out-group case. They are keyed with each tree's shipped build, as production keys them (evaluation-set-6c's amendment, decision 5).
- **Nothing generated is committed.** Lists, cases, customers, builds, observation files and every per-query result stay under `/tmp/openverify-eval-6c/`. Only aggregates are published here.
- **No tuning.** The matcher's author never saw this seed's output, and this report changes nothing it measures.

### The sets

1. **The 6B held-out set**, unchanged, so recall per variant source is comparable with `docs/calibration/watchlist-phase6.md`: 2,000 listed persons (a tenth listed in Cyrillic only), 600 listed companies, 500 held-out-group cases for each of the two group sources, and 10,000 person customers, in one list of about 3,600 entries. Each person case is screened by name only and with the listed birth date. The `typo-kinds` step splits its typo cases into substitutions, insertions, deletions and adjacent transpositions.
2. **The scale population (decision 5)**, drawn by `generateScalePopulation` from the performance tables' own heavy-headed name law, with the sizes fixed in code before any seed (`SCALE_SIZES`):
   - 1,050 listed persons, 150 for each source: `exact`, `order_swap`, `dropped_middle`, `added_middle`, `initials`, `typo` (one letter substituted, inserted or deleted), `transposition` (two adjacent letters swapped);
   - 400 listed companies, 100 for each source: `exact`, `typo`, `transposition`, `legal_form`;
   - 1,500 person customers and 500 company customers, never listed.

   The population is embedded in each performance table, so each scale table is the performance benchmark's own table (same generator, seed and profile) plus the population: one at **100,000** list names (sanctions-like, about 1.65 names per entry) and one at **2,000,000** (PEP-like, about 5 names per entry in several scripts). Every customer alert is a false alert, whether it matches a population entry or one of the table's own entries.
   - **Indistinguishable customers.** A person customer whose name equals some name of a listed individual of the table (as a folded token bag) that lists the customer's birth date, or a company customer whose name equals a listed entity's and, when the customer states a country, whose country agrees with one of the entity's, cannot be told apart from that entry by any screening on those fields. They are counted and reported apart, with how many alerted, and left out of the false-alert rate.

### How each query is screened

Through the production code path, as in 6B: a Testcontainers Postgres (`pgvector/pgvector:pg17` with the C73.8 flags), migrated with the tree's migrations; the list imported by `syncWatchlistSource` into the synthetic sandbox source; each query screened by the `watchlist` job (persons and person customers) or the `business-watchlist` job (companies and company customers) with the type's default configuration and `name-matching` set in turn to `exact`, `strict` and `tolerant`. A query's matches are the matches the job keeps. One query's database reads serve its three presets.

### Metrics

Per set, per preset and, for the 6B person cases, per query variant:

- **Recall per variant source**, and **recall over all cases** ("Recall, all cases"): the cases whose expected entry is among the kept matches, over the cases.
- **Precision:** the matches to the expected entry over all kept matches of the case queries. At scale a case's matches to the table's own entries count against it.
- **False alerts per 1,000 customers:** 1,000 times the customers with at least one kept match, over the customers (indistinguishable ones left out).
- **Diagnostics,** reported but not metrics: misses whose coverage stated a limit, searches cut short (`truncated`), errored runs.

### Intervals

- 2,000 bootstrap replicates over the listed identities and the customers, from one stream per set seeded by the evaluation seed, so the presets of a set are paired. At scale, the table's own entries are the fixed list under test: a match to one of them counts in every replicate.
- **Base and head** are drawn together (`benchmark:quality --step compare`): each replicate draws once and evaluates both runs, and the difference interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile (nearest rank) of `head - base`, replicate by replicate.
- A 95% Wilson interval is printed beside any count of zero or of all, where the bootstrap interval is degenerate.

### Performance (C78.7)

As 6B's method, on tables generated from this seed (`benchmark-tables.mjs`, Zipf with exponent 1 over 40,000 fictional names and the groups' Latin forms), imported through the sync, `VACUUM ANALYZE` before measuring:

- **One screening** is the `watchlist` job's screening with the default `tolerant` configuration and the tree's shipped variant groups, for a sandbox environment: the source state, retrieval, entry loads, scoring and coverage.
- **Subjects:** 150 listed entries' primary names with their birth dates and 150 unlisted names from the same law; 20 warm-ups, then a round over the 300.
- **Budgets:** 100 ms at 100,000 names and 250 ms at 2,000,000 (C78.7).
- **Cap and truncation** (first round): the screenings whose retrieval returned the full cap of 2,000 candidates ("the cap binds"); the screenings the search marked `truncated` (the cap, or at the head also a pair of tokens out of reach, decision 1); and the screenings that ended `errored` with `result_truncated` (cut short with nothing kept), against decision 1's target of at most 1% at 2,000,000 names. Wilson intervals beside each.
- **The re-screen** (C74.4, decision 3): the 100,000-name database with 200,000 recurring `watchlist` reports in one sandbox environment, then syncs adding 100, 300, 1,000 and 3,000 entries; after each, `processReportRescreen` is timed end to end and its inserted runs are reported. The target is 60 s at 1,000 changed entries and 200,000 subjects. **Run cost:** the runs inserted times the tree's own 100,000-name screening p50.
- **Unlisted alerts and the compacted round** (decision 7): `<scale>-unlisted` counts the first round's unlisted subjects with a kept `tolerant` match; `<scale>-compact` runs `VACUUM (FULL, ANALYZE)` and one more round. Both are reported as diagnostics, taken once.

### The timing rule

- Every timing runs under `flock /tmp/openverify-e2e.lock`, one step per lock hold, with `OPENVERIFY_C787_TIMING=assert` wherever the C78.7 gate runs.
- **A timing counts only when the 1-minute load average is at most 4 at its start and at its end** (`/proc/loadavg`). Otherwise it is discarded, and retaken when the machine is quieter.
- **Each timing is taken three times, and the median is reported** with the load averages:
  - **a screening take** is one measure round (one call with `--rounds 1`: 20 warm-ups, then the 300 subjects). Its start and end loads are the ones the step writes into the round file. The headline of a scale is the median of the three round p95s. The pooled p95 over the three rounds, 6B's headline, is printed beside it;
  - **a re-screen take** is one `rescreen-<size>` step on its own freshly built database (import, `rescreen-seed`, then the sizes in order), because a sync applied once cannot be applied again. Its loads are the ones the step writes before and after the re-screen. Three databases per tree are built for this (namespaces `-r2` and `-r3` for the second and third);
  - **a gate take** is one run of the C78.7 gate at the head (`watchlists.integration.test.ts`, the gate test alone).
- A take that never meets the load rule after repeated waits is reported with its loads and marked as not meeting it. No verdict rests on such a take alone.
- The unlisted counts, the cap counts, recall, precision and alerts do not depend on the machine. They are read from the first take.

### Decision rules

Each rule is fixed here, before the seed. "Paired interval" means the 95% interval of `head - base` on the same draws.

1. **Decision 1** (the absolute key bound, the pair keys, the fold of the rare-key lookup, the load and scoring shortcuts). **Kept** when both hold:
   - **recall is non-inferior** at both scales: for the persons and the companies of the scale population, at `exact`, `strict` and `tolerant`, the lower bound of the paired interval of "Recall, all cases" is above −1 percentage point (12 comparisons, every one must pass);
   - **latency improves** at both scales: the head's median round p95 is lower than the base's.

   Otherwise **revert**, naming the commits. Reported beside the verdict: the C78.7 p95 at both scales against its budget, how often the cap binds, the truncated share, and the `result_truncated` share against the 1% target at 2,000,000 names. A budget still missed is reported with its gap and is never a reason to change the budget. The 6B set's recall differences are reported too; that set is neither scale, so a lower bound below −1 point there is a finding for the owner, not a revert.

   Stage 1 changed several constants and rules together (the bound's share of the cap, the pair keys and their two limits, the reach rule, the trigram conditions, the frequencies counted over entries, the shortcuts, the analysed-name cache). The calibration varies none of them on its own, so each receives decision 1's verdict, with that limit stated.
2. **Decision 2** (the gate). The gate's numbers at the head, three takes. **Kept** when its output names the case `shipped-variant-groups` (the shipped groups present), its list follows the heavy-headed law, and its machine-independent checks pass. Its timing is compared with the head's 100,000-name measurement. A failed timing assertion is reported as such.
3. **Decision 3** (the forward screen). At 1,000 changed entries and 200,000 subjects, the head's re-screen time (median of three takes) against 60 s and against the base's, the runs inserted at each tree and their cost, and a **no-miss check** at the head:
   - after the head's first 1,000-entry sync, every one of the 200,000 subjects is screened in full, as its own run would screen it: its pinned configuration, against the whole table, with nothing persisted;
   - the subjects whose screening keeps a match on an entry that sync changed are counted, with how many of them the re-screen gave a `list_update` run. A **miss** is such a subject without a run.
   - No committed step does this, and adding one is outside this calibration's remit, so the check is a script outside git that calls only the committed helpers, the job and the repository. Its logic is stated here, its output is kept outside git, and committing it as a step is a follow-up.

   **Kept** when the check finds no miss and the head's re-screen is faster than the base's. **Revert** when a subject is missed or the head is slower. A time over 60 s is reported with its gap.
4. **Decision 4** (the optimal-string-alignment typo rule). **Reverted only on a material rise in false alerts** on the scale population: at either scale, for the person or the company customers, at `tolerant` (the only fixed preset with typos), the head's false alerts per 1,000 exceed the base's by more than 10% of the base's rate, **and** the paired interval of the difference lies above zero. Otherwise kept. Transposition recall (the scale sets' `transposition` source and the 6B set's transposition typos) is reported beside it. The head also changes retrieval (decision 1), so a rise may come from either change. The rule is applied as written, and the rates at `exact` and `strict`, which no typo rule touches, are reported beside it for attribution.
5. **Decision 5** (the set). **Kept** when, at both scales, precision and false alerts per 1,000 are measurable at each type's default preset (`tolerant` for persons, `strict` for companies). Measurable means non-degenerate: at least one false alert and at least one customer without an alert, and at least one expected and one other match, so the bootstrap intervals have a positive width. Every preset is reported, and the indistinguishable customers are counted apart.

### What these sets do not cover

- Real names, real lists, real customers and real data entry. Every variation is a rule applied to fictional names and to name forms combined at random.
- Name frequencies other than the assumed Zipf law (exponent 1). Real lists and customer bases have their own, and the alert rate depends on them.
- Scripts beyond the generators': the 6B set's cross-script cases use the held-out groups' scripts and Cyrillic; the scale population is in Latin script. Han, Thai and other scripts are compared only through lossy forms, which coverage flags.
- Variants the generators do not make: misspellings beyond one edit, romanisations outside the groups and ICAO's tables, nicknames, joined or split tokens at scale (the 6B set has them).
- PEP positions, classes and relatives, adverse media, identifiers, vessels, aircraft, crypto addresses, weak aliases and the `custom` preset.
- Lists of other sizes. The alert rate grows with the list's size and with how common its names are.
- One machine, shared with other work. Timings depend on it; the load rule bounds how much.

## Amendments

Made after the method was committed. None changes a metric, a set, a rule or a margin.

1. **No benchmark code was changed.** Every step ran as committed. Three ways of running them are recorded here:
   - **The 2,000,000-name quality import** ran with `NODE_OPTIONS=--max-old-space-size=12288`, the heap that the performance CLI's script entry already carries, because its sync parses a 450 MB file. The quality CLI's entry does not carry it (a follow-up).
   - **Some calls ran past ten minutes.** The 2,000,000-name `scale-screen` at the base checks its 8-minute budget only after each 200-query batch, so each call ran 11 minutes. A few other steps also waited that long for the shared lock. The tool then moved the call to the background, and each was waited for before the next step began. No result was lost: each step writes its observations as it goes and resumes from them. A per-query budget check is a follow-up.
   - **Load waits.** Before each timing, the runner waited inside the lock for the 1-minute load to fall below 2 to 2.5, so that the step's own load would not carry the end of the take over 4.
2. **Discarded takes** (the timing rule):
   - The base's third 100,000-name round was taken four times. It ended at 4.51, started at 5.09, ended at 4.56, then passed at 2.40 → 2.16.
   - The head's third 100,000-name round was taken twice. It started at 4.57, then passed at 2.48 → 2.25.
   - The discarded round files are kept apart, outside git, and are not used.
3. **Re-screens.** The head's first 100-entry take ended at load 6.85, so that size has two valid head takes. The 3,000-entry size was taken once per tree, in the first database only: the method's three takes concern the 1,000-entry target, and the 100- and 300-entry syncs come along in each take. The base's 3,000-entry take started at 6.59, so it does not meet the rule and is shown marked.
4. **"The cap binds"** is counted from the first round's files: the screenings whose retrieval returned 2,000 candidates. The step's own "cut at the cap" column counts `truncated`, which at the head also includes a pair of tokens out of reach. Both are shown.
5. **Two runs not in the method,** each reported as context, not as a verdict input:
   - the base's own C78.7 gate, run once on the same machine and day, for decision 2;
   - the final reproduction of the head's 100,000-name quality and performance steps, from an empty directory (see "Reproduction").
6. **The decision 3 check** ran as fixed in the method: a script outside git (kept in the calibration's scratch directory) that imports the head worktree's `benchmark-postgres.mjs`, its `watchlist` job and its repository. It screened all 200,000 subjects in three processes in one lock hold per call, five calls in all, after the head's first 1,000-entry sync and before its 3,000-entry sync.

**What the scale population shows.**
- **Recall rises or holds everywhere.** The pair keys and the out-of-reach lookup find the dropped and added middle names and the initials that the base's rare keys missed. At 100,000 names, `strict` persons go from 144 to 150 of 150 (dropped middle) and from 138 to 150 (initials). The OSA rule adds the transpositions: at `tolerant` persons go from 0 to 129 of 150 at 100,000 names and to 127 at 2,000,000, and companies go from 0 to 50 of 100.
- **Typos at 2,000,000 names:** 117 of 150 at the base and 116 at the head (−0.7 points, −4.5 to +3.2). This is the one source where the head finds a case fewer, within the margin. matcher-6c saw the same on its development tables: when a common given name is over the bound and the misspelt family name has no key of the listed entry, only trigrams can reach it.
- **False alerts are high on a heavy-headed law.** At `tolerant`, 17.4% of person customers alert against 100,000 names at the head, and 46.2% against 2,000,000. At `exact`, 6.5% and 17.7% do: namesakes whose name equals a listed name, with another birth date or none listed. The head's falls come from retrieval: the rates fall at `strict` too, where no typo rule acts.
- **Precision falls at 100,000 names** (persons at `tolerant`: 39.3% to 32.9%). The head keeps more matches for the same 1,050 listed cases (3,055 against 2,176), most of them other entries with the same or a similar name. At 2,000,000 names precision is about 2% at both trees: a listed person's query keeps about 37 matches on average.
- **Errored runs are not alerts.** A search cut short with nothing kept ends `errored` (`result_truncated`), which a reviewer must still look at. At 2,000,000 names at `tolerant`, 74 cases and 228 person customers errored at the base, and 3 and 11 at the head. So the base's alert rate there is understated, and the head's fall is not an artefact of errors.
- **Companies are identical at both scales.** The performance tables hold individuals only, so a company customer can only meet the population's 400 listed companies. The company rates therefore measure a 400-entry list at either scale.

## Findings

Each is for its owner. None was acted on here: the calibration changes no constant, rule, budget or code (H21).

1. **Two C78.7 budgets and the re-screen target are still missed** (the owner, decision 1; `matcher-6c`'s options).
   - **100,000 names:** 109.5 ms against 100 ms. The head sits at the budget: the reproduction at a lower load gave 93.0 ms.
   - **2,000,000 names:** 399.4 ms against 250 ms. Scoring and coverage hold 301.6 ms of the p95, on namesake floods: an unlisted subject keeps 19 matches on average at this scale (2,904 kept matches for 150 subjects).
   - **The re-screen:** 95.1 s against 60 s at 1,000 changed entries.

   The budgets are not moved. Decision 1 says the owner revisits them with these gaps.
2. **The re-screen is slower than the base at small syncs** (`matcher-6c` or `fix-6c`). It takes 53.2 s at 100 added entries against 25.2 s, and 63.4 s at 300 against 42.7 s, over the 60 s target where the base was within it. Small syncs are the common case of a daily list. The cost looks mostly fixed: 53 s at 100 entries and 63 s at 300 put about 48 s before the first entry. This calibration does not measure where it goes; the reverse index of the 200,000 subjects, keyed from the source's frequencies, is a likely place to look. The head still costs less once the runs it causes are counted (2,654 runs at the base against 171 at 100 entries).
3. **The gate passes on a lighter list than the calibration's** (the owner, decision 2). It measured 75.4 ms with a candidates' p95 of 503, while the same head measured 109.5 ms with 630 on `benchmark-tables.mjs`' 100,000-name table. The gate is the right kind of test now, but a pass is not evidence that the budget is met at the calibration's scale and law.
4. **False alerts on common names are high.** This is the plan's first risk, now measured. At the default `tolerant`, 17.4% of person customers alert against 100,000 names and 46.2% against 2,000,000 under a Zipf law of exponent 1. At 2,000,000 names a listed person's own query keeps about 37 entries, a precision of 2.4%. These rates depend on the assumed law, but they are what reviewers would see on such a list.
5. **The bootstrap interval of the company rates does not cover its own point estimate** (`evaluation-set-6c` or `fix-6c`, `benchmark-paired.mjs`). A replicate's list holds only the identities it drew, about 63% of them. A customer whose matches are all to resampled identities therefore alerts in only part of the replicates, and a false match counts only when its entry was drawn.
   - For persons, most false alerts are to the table's own entries, which count in every replicate, so the intervals cover their points.
   - For companies, every false alert is to a population entry. So, for example, companies at `strict` give 247.9 per 1,000 with an interval of 148.8 to 235.5, and a precision of 37.3% with an interval of 39.4 to 60.7%.

   The decision rules read differences, and the company differences here are exactly zero, so no verdict depends on these intervals. The points and the Wilson intervals are correct. To fix the interval, a customer's matches to population entries could count as part of the fixed list under test, or the company rates could carry Wilson intervals only. That is a change of the interval's definition, for its owner, followed by a new calibration.

   **Since the Workflow 6C review (C002):** until that change, `benchmark:render` marks every scale-table interval that excludes its own point ("which excludes the point"), prints the Wilson interval beside it and says why under the table. The company tables above carry those marks, rendered from this run's result files; no number changed.
6. **Company customers meet only 400 listed companies at either scale** (`evaluation-set-6c`, the owner). The performance tables hold individuals only (`benchmark-tables.mjs` writes `kind: 'individual'`), so the company rates are the same at 100,000 and 2,000,000 names and do not measure a large company list. Entities in the tables would need a new table law and a new calibration.
7. **`result_truncated` at `strict` and `exact`** (the owner). At 2,000,000 names the head errors 34 of 2,549 scale-population screenings at `strict` (1.33%, Wilson 0.96–1.86%) and 83 at `exact` (3.26%), above the 1% target that the default `tolerant` meets (0.55%). A truncated search errors only when it keeps nothing, and the stricter presets keep less. The base errored 446 and 798.
8. **The benchmark's own gaps** (`fix-6c`, `packages/watchlist`):
   - the quality CLI's script entry lacks `--max-old-space-size=12288`, which its 2,000,000-name import needs;
   - `scale-screen` checks its budget once per 200-query batch, so a call runs 11 minutes at the base's 2,000,000 names;
   - the reads wrapper records `truncated` but not `capReached` and `unreachable`, so the performance summary's "cut at the cap" counts searches out of reach at the head;
   - the no-miss check of decision 3 is not a committed step.
