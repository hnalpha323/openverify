# Plan: Phase 5, other verifications

**Source:** `docs/PLAN.md` §4.3 (the 31 checks of documents, databases, businesses, crypto wallets, contact details and NFC chips) and §6 Phase 5; ADR 0002 (tiers and honesty); four research notes taken on 2026-10-21 for this plan, committed with this plan as `docs/research/phase5-inventory.md`, `phase5-libraries.md`, `phase5-nfc.md` and `phase5-behaviour.md` (clean-room notes in our own words; the NFC spike scripts, their fixtures and the four downloaded master lists stay out of git); the inventory summarised under "Starting point", re-checked against the code at `3cfabd1` and again, for the review, at `440d5cf`.
**Complexity:** Very large. Three Workflows of ten agents each:
- **5A** opens every layer to the nine new verification types (Stage 1a `contracts`, Stage 1b `foundation`), then builds phone and email OTP, crypto wallets, the shared matching library, and database and AAMVA verification;
- **5B** builds documents and proof of address (PDF forensics), KYB (business registry and business website) and server-side NFC chip verification;
- **5C** adds the console surfaces, reviews all of Phase 5 and fixes.

**Why this split.** The matching library (names with nicknames, addresses, partial dates, match groups) serves database and AAMVA, the document comparisons and KYB, so it lands in 5A. Contact and crypto need no vision change and no PKI, so they share 5A with the plumbing. 5B carries the three heavy additions (the PDF stack, the eMRTD PKI package, the external registries and RDAP) and reuses 5A's matching library and inputs route. 5C needs every type's API documents. Stage 1 of 5A is split in two because one agent cannot finish nine types across every layer green (F03).

**Review of this plan.** Three adversarial reviews read the first draft: privacy and security (P01–P27), check honesty and product coverage (H01–H37), feasibility and fit with the code (F01–F24). Together they found 1 CRITICAL, 26 HIGH, 35 MEDIUM and 26 LOW, 88 in all; `docs/plans/phase-5-review-resolution.md` disposes of each. Among them:
- a default NFC verification passed on copied chip data, a client could withhold DG15 and the AA response unnoticed, and a gap in the trust store failed genuine passports as fraud;
- no new PII attribute was in the fixed masking lists, and the planned log keys would have blanked every logged error;
- PDF and JPEG 2000 parsing had resource limits only, on the network the data stores share; the trust-store sync fetched CRLs with no SSRF guard and never withdrew an anchor; the registry cache stored officers unsealed;
- missing fields, smart-contract wallets, expired documents, unrecognised documents, new domains and pasted signature slips failed as fraud; send and challenge caps left users stuck; invisible PDF text satisfied recency; five fabrication cues fire on genuine PDFs;
- the silent AAMVA step could be skipped before any ID existed, and a skip never settled the inquiry;
- Stage 1 left no extension points in the API's shared files, the OpenAPI snapshot would break every parallel API agent, and 5A Stage 1 was too large for one agent.

The main changes:
- **NFC** (C62): data integrity is judged apart from revocation; `failed` only for proven tampering, a trust-store gap or issuer defect ends in review; the signed SOD's data-group list tells "withheld" from "absent"; the DG2-to-selfie and DG1-to-printed-MRZ bindings are required by default, and a new SOD-reuse check records selfie-bound sightings;
- **PII and logs** (C53.7, C53.8): one PII attribute list in contracts feeds both masking lists, with tests per exit; specific log keys;
- **isolation** (C60.2, C54.6, C62.7): PDF and JPEG 2000 parsing in a sandboxed child with no network, vision on an internal network; one outbound HTTP client with a fixed private-network refusal, no redirects and size caps; a trust-store sync with CRL rollback checks and anchor withdrawal;
- **honest outcomes:** three-valued match groups and defined address levels (C58), contract-wallet and Solana-PDA paths (C57), a document cascade, visible-text-only extraction and corrected fabrication cues (C60), LEI status mapping and domain age only for registered domains, with a resolution check as the website's required core (C61), caps that submit or cancel instead of stranding the user (C55, C57, C62);
- **feasibility:** per-type API hooks and frozen route schemas (C54), migrations 0016–0018, a `fixtures-5b` agent, one client agent in 5B, three dashboard agents in 5C.

One finding was rejected with evidence (H27); one part of another was deferred (H31a).

A fourth pass checked the revision against every finding. It confirmed 79 resolved, the rejection and the deferral, finished the 7 partly resolved items, and found 23 more problems (no CRITICAL, 3 HIGH, 12 MEDIUM, 8 LOW), all now fixed (`docs/plans/phase-5-review-resolution.md`, "Verification round"):
- the required NFC repeat check failed genuine users as fraud (every inquiry without a reference id gets a new account, and a failed replay still recorded a sighting): it is now advisory, counts only selfie-bound sightings, and answers review-level (C62.6);
- a reviewed passport skipped AAMVA, and a later US licence then passed without it: AAMVA attempts are bound to the ID verification they came from, and an eligible decision after a server skip re-opens it (C59.6);
- no Stage 3 agent owned its `packages/db` repositories or the config files: Stage 1 creates each with its export line and parses every variable, and each builder owns its own files (C54.4, the Workflows);
- and twelve MEDIUM: a hand-written seccomp denylist (replaced by network isolation and stated layers, C60.2), OTP limits that stranded users, the settings field without an owner, the publish rules without a builder, parallel edits to `check-config.ts`, the per-service secrets against the startup checks, a fraud name comparison on household bills, one recency window for leases and tax forms, one-attempt KYB, a contradictory specimen test, an e2e case that could not pass, and an AAMVA predicate with a gap.

## Summary

Phase 4 left two verification types runnable. Core knows nine (`VERIFICATION_TYPES`), the registry holds all 31 §4.3 checks and flow-spec has steps for five of them, but every layer above core is closed to `government_id` and `selfie`. Phase 5 makes every §4.3 check, and seven new ones, real at its tier (ADR 0002):
- **R:** real logic, deterministic or on an open model's output;
- **H:** a heuristic with a confidence, shipped as `beta`;
- **A:** an adapter with a deterministic sandbox provider; without a bound provider the check answers `not_applicable`/`provider_not_configured`, never `passed`.

What lands:
- **Plumbing** (C50–C54): the type split that matches the public identifiers (`aamva`, `database-business`, `business-website`), an explicit step-to-type map, default attempt counts, a generic route for entered values, PDF and chip-file uploads, a check `category`, `confirmed` and `skipped` wired, sealed storage and masking for every new PII value, and per-type extension points in the API.
- **Contact** (C55, C56): our own OTP (HMAC under an HKDF key from the KEK, 10-minute codes, attempt and guess budgets, resend limits, SMS-pumping controls), sent from the API process through the existing mailer and a new SMS port (a Twilio adapter, a sandbox sender with a sandbox-only sink), plus disposable-domain and MX checks.
- **Crypto wallets** (C57): address validity for eight chains and ownership by a server-issued, single-use challenge (EIP-191 over EIP-4361, BIP-137, BIP-322 simple, TRON, Solana ed25519), signed through an injected EIP-1193 wallet or pasted.
- **Matching** (C58) and **database and AAMVA** (C59): nicknames, a libpostal-style address normaliser, partial birthdates and three-valued match groups as real logic; the records come from adapters with sandbox providers; a silent AAMVA step run by the worker.
- **Documents** (C60): `/v1/analyze/document` with PDF parsing in a sandboxed subprocess (pypdfium2, pypdf), a lexicon and layout classifier, extraction from visible text, recency and expiry, and one `beta` fabrication score.
- **KYB** (C61): GLEIF on by default, Companies House and SEC EDGAR when configured, a domain-resolution check, RDAP domain age, and a malicious-website adapter whose feed list ships empty.
- **NFC on the server** (C62): `@openverify/emrtd` re-runs Passive Authentication and verifies Active Authentication over a server nonce, against an operator-synced trust store that is never committed; reuse of a chip's SOD across accounts is detected now. The Android reader stays Phase 10; tests build synthetic chips.
- **Clients and console** (C64, C66): every step except NFC in the web flow and on Android; the console evidence for every type.

mdoc and verifiable credentials stay a later stretch goal (C68).

## Amendment from the Kotlin Multiplatform migration

*Recorded on 2026-10-22 by the migration's Stage 4 (`kmp-integration`), from ADR 0004 and `docs/plans/kmp-migration.plan.md`. It also amends C64.2, C52.7, Stage 1a's Android strings, the `android-5a` and `clients-5b` bullets and the Validation list in place. Stage 1a re-verifies these facts with the rest of the Starting point.*

The owner asked for a language "that we can easily port to iOS alongside Android". Before Phase 5 adds Android code, `android/openverify-sdk` therefore became one Kotlin Multiplatform library: Android, `iosArm64` and `iosSimulatorArm64`, with Compose Multiplatform. Its packages, namespace, artifact and public Android API are unchanged, except that `FlowApiClient(…, OkHttpClient)` is now a factory function (KMP decision 7). `demo-app` and `openverify-nfc` are unchanged. For the Android work of 5A and 5B:

- **Where code goes.**
  - `src/commonMain/kotlin/dev/openverify/sdk/` holds `model`, `mrz`, `flow` (`FlowCoordinator`), `api` (`FlowApi`, and `FlowApiClient` on Ktor 3.5.2), `InquiryViewModel`, `InquiryOutcomes` and every screen in `ui/`. A new step screen goes to `ui/steps/<Type>Step.kt`, routed from `InquiryFlowScreen`.
  - `src/androidMain` holds only what needs Android: `OpenVerifyInquiry` (the `ActivityResultContract` entry points), the activity, `InquiryResults`, CameraX, image encoding, the system photo picker and the camera permission. C64.2's `DocumentPicker` is a common interface the host passes in, implemented here.
  - `src/iosMain` holds actuals only. A new platform need becomes an interface the host passes in, or a minimal `expect`/`actual` with an `androidMain` actual and an `iosMain` placeholder that never claims a capability, as `CameraPermission.Unavailable` does.
  - `commonMain` imports no OkHttp, `android.*`, `java.*` or `R`. Each `commonMain` dependency publishes `iosArm64` and `iosSimulatorArm64` variants, and `:openverify-sdk` uses no cinterop.
- **Network.** A new `FlowApi` method goes into the common `FlowApiClient`, through `FlowHttp`: `jsonBody` for JSON, explicit `PartData` parts for multipart (quoted names, and the file part's `Content-Length`). It gets MockEngine cases in `commonTest/…/api/FlowApiClientContractTest` and, where the Android wire matters, MockWebServer cases in `androidHostTest/…/api/FlowApiClientTest`. KMP K4's rules hold: no redirect followed, no `ContentNegotiation`, a `kotlinx.io.IOException` becomes `Network`, and cancellation is never caught.
- **Strings.** They are Compose Multiplatform resources in `src/commonMain/composeResources/values{,-es}/strings_<type>.xml`, read with `Res.string` or `Res.plurals` through `stringResource` or `pluralStringResource` (`getString` outside a composition). Keys keep the `openverify_` prefix.
  - Compose Multiplatform 1.11 fills only positional arguments (`%1$s`, `%1$d`) and reads only the `\n`, `\t`, `\uXXXX` and `\\` escapes. Write `'` and `"` as they are.
  - `ResourcesTest` refuses a key missing from either language, any other format specifier (`%d`, `%.1f`, `%%`), Spanish placeholders that differ from the English ones, and Android escapes such as `\'`. `SpanishMessagesTest` refuses a Spanish text equal to its English one. Lint's `MissingTranslation` no longer reads these strings.
- **Consent locale.** The consent screen sends the language of the texts it shows (C37) through `FlowActions.acceptConsent(optionalTextKeys, locale)`. `FlowCoordinator.Settings.locale` is only a fallback.
- **Tests.**
  - `src/androidHostTest` (JUnit 5, Robolectric, Compose UI) holds the former `src/test` suites, `ContractFixturesTest` included, which reads `contracts/fixtures` from the classpath as before.
  - `src/commonTest` (kotlin.test, Ktor MockEngine, coroutines-test) holds the tests of new pure code. It runs inside `testAndroidHostTest` now, and on the iOS simulator from Phase 10.
  - The two compile into one JVM test compilation, so a `commonTest` class must not share a name with an `androidHostTest` class of the same package.
  - Robolectric tests that drive a real `InquiryViewModel` wait with `waitForIdle()`, because the view model calls the server on the main looper.
- **Tasks and gates.** `testDebugUnitTest` no longer reaches the SDK: its tests run in `testAndroidHostTest`. `:openverify-sdk:assemble` builds the AAR and compiles the iOS main and test klibs on Linux. CI fails under 631 SDK host tests or on any skipped one. Kover's 80% gate measures `commonMain` and `androidMain`.

### C55. Phone and email OTP

1. **Who sends.** The API process, on the flow request: it holds the mailer and the SMS port. The worker never sends, so it needs no mailer. A delivery failure reaches the user at once.
2. **Code.** `crypto.randomInt(0, 10 ** length)`, zero-padded; `codeLength` 6–10, default 6 (SP 800-63B-4 §3.1.3.2; ASVS 5.0.0 6.5.4).
3. **Key and hash.** `K_otp = HKDF-SHA-256(KEK, salt empty, info "openverify/otp/v1", 32)` per KEK of the keyring (`otpKey(keyring, kid)`); `code_key_id` records the KEK; stored `HMAC-SHA-256(K_otp, "otp/v1|" + verificationId + "|" + channel + "|" + normalisedDestination + "|" + code)`, compared with `timingSafeEqual`. A code whose KEK has left the keyring reads as `expired` (the user resends), logged without ids.
4. **Lifetime and use.** 600 s (ASVS 6.5.5); single use; bound to verification, channel and destination; a send to another destination supersedes the open code. After `confirmed`, a send or confirm answers 409 `otp_already_confirmed`.
5. **Attempts** (P09). Confirm reserves an attempt first with `UPDATE … SET failed_attempts = failed_attempts + 1 WHERE status = 'sent' AND failed_attempts < 5 AND expires_at > $now RETURNING`, then compares; on a match it confirms the row and moves the verification. The service returns a result object, so the counted attempt commits with the 422 (`otp_incorrect`, with `attempts-remaining`). 5 attempts exhaust a code (`otp_code_exhausted`). 10 wrong entries across a verification's codes (counted in `otp_verification_states.wrong_entries`; a match releases its reservation, so a correct first code counts no wrong entry; a new code never resets the count) submit it in the same transaction; the check fails `otp_attempts_exhausted` (user behaviour, retryable). A destination guess budget, `otp-destination-guess-day` (20 wrong codes per destination and channel per environment per day, GCRA, fail closed), stops guessing across inquiries; when it trips, confirms and sends to that destination answer 429 `otp_rate_limited`.
6. **Sends.** At most 5 per verification; cooldowns after sends 1–4 of 30, 60, 120, 240 s (`429 otp_resend_too_soon` with `resend-available-at`). Order (P11): validate; read the verification's count and cooldown without a lock; consume the GCRA limits; then lock (C53.6), re-check, insert (superseding), commit; then send; then a conditional `UPDATE … WHERE id = $1 AND status = 'sent'` under the lock order records `provider_message_id` or `errored` (`502 otp_delivery_failed`); an errored send does not count toward the cap. **No dead end** (H07, V11): a send request after the cap, or any request after the last code expired with no sends left, submits the verification, and the check fails `otp_not_confirmed` (user behaviour), so the client offers "start again" within `maxAttempts`. A refusal by a destination, inquiry, guess or environment limit, or a 503 while Valkey is down, is recorded in `otp_verification_states.limited_at`; the client may then submit, and the check answers `otp_delivery_unavailable` (review). A test per limit.
7. **Limits** (provisional; `OTP_LIMITS`, a closed set outside `RATE_LIMIT_POLICIES`, a C12 amendment; `RateLimitRule.name` widens to `RateLimitPolicy | OtpLimitName`). Per environment: `otp-destination-hour` 5 and `otp-destination-day` 10 sends; `otp-ip-hour` 10 sends per IPv4 address or IPv6 /64 (P10); `otp-inquiry-day` 10; `otp-sms-prefix-hour` 20 per first 8 E.164 digits; `otp-sms-country-hour`, at most half the budget per destination country, applied only when the step allows more than one country; and the environment setting `otp-sms-max-per-hour` (a C16 setting added to the settings schemas, defaults and update body in Stage 1a, resolved and served by Stage 1b, enforced by `api-otp`; 1 up to the install ceiling `OPENVERIFY_OTP_SMS_MAX_PER_HOUR_CEILING`, default 1,000; default 200). Every refusal is one `429 otp_rate_limited` with `Retry-After`. OTP limits **fail closed** (503) when Valkey is down. When the environment budget trips, the API writes one audit-log entry per hour (`environment.otp-sms-budget-tripped`, an audit action added in Stage 1a), and the settings resource serves the latest one as read-only `otp-sms-budget-tripped-at` (V08). Limits are tested against real Valkey (it reads its own clock), recovery with a short rule; cooldowns and expiry use the application clock. At startup, with an SMS sender bound and `trustProxy` false, the API logs once that per-IP limits key on the proxy's address; `docs/checks/contact.md` explains the setting.
8. **Destinations.** Phone: `libphonenumber-js@1.13.14` with `/max` metadata; `isValid()` required; the region in `allowedCountries`; `PREMIUM_RATE`, `SHARED_COST`, `TOLL_FREE`, `UAN`, `PAGER`, `VOICEMAIL` refused; `VOIP` unless `allowVoip`. Email: the WHATWG pattern, RFC 5321 lengths, a dot in the domain, `url.domainToASCII`, SMTPUTF8 local parts refused; the index over the lower-cased address. Only validated addresses reach nodemailer, as `{ address }` objects; a test feeds the three 2026-10-19 advisory payloads and asserts each is refused before nodemailer.
9. **Messages** (en, es). SMS: `"<org>: <code> is your verification code. Do not share it.\n\n@<flow host> #<code>"`. Email: subject `"<org> verification code"`, plain text, no links. `<org>` is the organization name with control characters, `@`, `#`, URLs and runs of 4 or more digits stripped, at most 40 characters. `<flow host>` comes from `OPENVERIFY_FLOW_PUBLIC_URL`, never from `Host` or `Origin` (tested).
10. **Channels.** `SmsSender` `{ id, sandbox, send({ to, body }) → { providerMessageId? } }`, bound by `OPENVERIFY_PROVIDER_SMS_{SANDBOX,PRODUCTION}`: `twilio` (the Messaging API through the outbound client; `OPENVERIFY_TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID`, `_API_KEY_SID`, `_API_KEY_SECRET`, and `_MESSAGING_SERVICE_SID` or `_FROM`; tested against a local stub) or `sandbox` (sandbox kind only: sends nothing, stores `{ destination index, code, sent-at }` in the Valkey list `openverify:sandbox-sms:<environment id>`, at most 100 entries, 900 s TTL). Email uses the mailer (`Mailer.configured`). Publishing a `phone_otp` step without an SMS sender for the environment's kind, or an `email_otp` step without a mailer, answers 422 `otp_channel_not_configured`.
11. **The sandbox sink** (P13). `POST /api/v1/sandbox/sms-messages/search` with `{ "phone-number" }` in the body (never in a URL), scope `sandbox:read` (a new scope, additive), answers `{ data: [{ code, sent-at }] }` for that number, newest first; the member is named `code`, so the stored API log redacts it (`API_LOG_REDACTED_KEYS`), with a test (V22). The plugin is registered only when the process has the sandbox sender bound and is not in production mode; a production environment's key gets 404. Tests: absent from a production-mode `/openapi.json`; no number in the request log.
12. **Logs and events.** The code never appears in logs, events, problems or flow documents (C53.8). No new event.

3. **Submit.** `{ "crypto": { "challenge-id", "signature", "signed-message"?, "scheme"? } }` (`eip191`, `bip137`, `bip322-simple`, `bip322-legacy`, `solana-ed25519`, `tron-v2`); consumed by a conditional update; one 409 `crypto_challenge_invalid` body for every refusal.
4. **Checks.**
   - validity: the claimed address for its chain;
   - disallowed chain: `allowed-chains`, else `no_allowlist`;
   - address (user behaviour): the signature proves control of the claimed address. EVM: a 65-byte signature with `v` in {0, 1, 27, 28} that recovers to the claimed address passes; otherwise, with `evmRpc` bound, `eth_getCode` decides: no code fails `signature_address_mismatch`; code goes to ERC-1271 `isValidSignature` (one call per submit, `gas` 500,000, result exactly 32 bytes equal to `0x1626ba7e` padded), which passes only with `allow-contract-wallets` or an equal `expected-address`, else `contract_wallet_not_allowed` (review); with `evmRpc` unbound, `provider_not_configured` (ADR 0002 §3e; review). Unknown wallet envelopes give `signature_inconclusive`;
   - statement: passes only when the address check verified the signature over exactly the bytes compared with the challenge (after the Solana off-chain v1 unwrap), else `input_missing`;
   - flags: `high-s`, `bip137-loose`, `contract-wallet`, `checksum: absent`, `on-curve`.

   Docs: a contract wallet proves control of a contract, not of a person; ownership means control of the key at signing time; without an RPC, a wrong-account EVM signature goes to review.
5. **`evmRpc`.** `json-rpc` (own `eth_call` and `eth_getCode` through the outbound client, URLs per chain in `OPENVERIFY_EVM_RPC_URLS`, 10 s) or `sandbox` (the address `0x00000000000000000000000000000000000c0de5`, case-insensitive, is a contract whose owner key is a fixed test key; a 65-byte owner signature recovers to the owner, not the contract). No public RPC ships.
6. **Vectors.** BIP-173/350 lists, BIP-322 basic and generated vectors, EIP-55's eight cases, the EIP-4361 examples, a Safe-shaped 65-byte owner signature (`provider_not_configured` without RPC, `passed` with the sandbox RPC and `allow-contract-wallets`), an off-curve Solana key; dev-only oracles `bitcoin-address-validation@4.0.0`, `micro-eth-signer@0.20.1`.

### C58. The matching library

`packages/checks/src/matching/`, used by database, AAMVA forms, document comparisons and KYB; government ID comparisons are unchanged.
1. **Levels and groups.** `MatchLevel`: `full`, `partial`, `none`, `missing` (no data on one side, or values in two scripts: never a mismatch). Results are `{ level, methods }` with the public method ids (`remove_prefixes`, `remove_suffixes`, `remove_special_characters`, `fold_characters`, `string_similarity`, `string_difference`, `nickname`, `substring`, `tokenization`, `date_similarity`, `date_difference`). `evaluateMatchGroups` gives each group **met** (every field at its level), **unmet** (some field `none` or below its level) or **undecidable** (none below, some `missing`). An identity check passes when any group is met, fails only when every group is unmet, and otherwise answers `insufficient_data` (review). Tests cover a step that collects name and phone, a record without a birthdate, and a business that left both optional inputs blank.
2. **Names.** Fold, strip titles, join or split particles, swapped given and family names (`partial`), initials (`partial`), nicknames (`partial`, `nickname`), token Jaro-Winkler ≥ 0.92 (`partial`); `full` only on equal folded tokens. Generational suffixes and legal forms are stripped only when one side lacks one; when both carry different ones the level is at most `partial` with a flag. Two scripts give `missing` with `script_mismatch`.
3. **Nicknames.** Our own table `packages/checks/data/nicknames.json` (≈ 1,200–1,800 pairs), each row with its source (a Wikidata QID or `authored`), generated from a Wikidata query whose text, date and result SHA-256 are recorded beside the table, and reviewed by the agent; committed as authored data.
4. **Addresses.** A libpostal-style normaliser with dictionaries seeded from libpostal `resources/dictionaries` (MIT; `all/given_names.txt` and OSM-wiki files excluded) and OpenCage `address-formatting/conf` (MIT), postal patterns from libaddressinput (CC-BY 4.0, attributed), each source pinned in `data/manifest.json` (added by `checks-matching`) and the generated tables committed. Levels: `full` when country, postcode, house number, street and city match and the unit is equal or absent on both; `partial` when country, house number and street match plus postcode or city, allowing a one-sided unit or one fuzzy component; `none` when both sides carry a house number or a postcode and they differ, or the street does not match; `missing` without a comparable component. Tests: a neighbour's number is `none`; a one-sided unit is `partial`.
5. **Partial dates.** R1 exact (`full`), R2 precision-limited (`partial`), R3 range or circa (`partial`), R4 transposition (`partial`, `transposed`; `birthdate_ambiguous` unless accepted), R5 one-digit or swapped-digit typo (`none`, `near-match`; stricter than the public 2-of-3 rule, recorded in C50.9), R6 two-digit-year century.
6. **Business names.** Legal-form canonicalisation (as C58.2 for differing forms), `&` ↔ `and`, a leading "The", punctuation.
7. **Input hygiene** (P27). Every matcher takes capped input (names 200, addresses 500, document text 600 lines of 300 characters per page); `eslint-plugin-regexp` (MIT, dev) refuses backtracking-prone patterns in the new folders; a timing test feeds 100 KB of near-matching input to each matcher. Jaro-Winkler vectors are extended offline from `jellyfish 1.2.1` and `rapidfuzz 3.14.6`.

### C60. Documents and proof of address

1. **Vision `POST /v1/analyze/document`** (per-route limit 1 × upload + 64 KiB). Measurements only: `source`, `page_count`, `analysed_pages` (the first three and the last), per page `width`, `height`, `text_layer` (lines with boxes, font ids and **`visible`**, false for render mode 3, clipped or off-page, covered by an opaque fill, fill equal to the background, or under 2 pt high), `ocr` (≤ 600 lines of 300 characters), `text_layer_ocr_agreement`; `pdf` (`producer`, `creator`, `creation_date`, `modification_date`, `xmp` fields from a bounded byte scan, `revisions` excluding the first-page section a `/Linearized` dictionary declares, `xref_repaired`, `eof_markers`, `bytes_after_last_eof`, `object_streams`, `fonts`, `annotations`, `form_fields`, `actions` (JavaScript, Launch, SubmitForm, GoToR, RichMedia, EmbeddedFile only; a GoTo `/OpenAction` is not counted), `signatures`, `edits_after_signature` excluding DSS-only and DocTimeStamp-only increments); `image` (`exif_software`, EXIF dates, JPEG double compression and quality, `ela_text_cell_score`, `copy_move_score`); `model_versions`. Errors: 415, 413, 422 `pdf_encrypted`, 422 `pdf_unprocessable`, 422 `too_many_pages` (> 50), and 503 when the worker was killed or timed out (the job retries, then `execution_error`).
2. **The PDF and image guard** (P04, V15; decision 48). No seccomp filter ships: a hand-written denylist is bypassable (the i386 ABI, `io_uring`, `execveat`), a correct allowlist for a Python process running PDFium is fragile, and libseccomp is LGPL (rule 4 allows LGPL only in `android/openverify-nfc`). Three layers instead, each stated with its limit in `docs/checks/document.md`:
   - **The child.** Every PDF and every JPEG 2000 decode runs in a fresh child (`python -I -m openverify_vision.guarded.worker`, `env={}`, bytes on stdin) that imports only the standard library, pypdfium2, pypdf and Pillow (a test asserts no numpy, OpenCV or onnxruntime is loaded) and sets `RLIMIT_AS` 1.5 GiB, `RLIMIT_CPU` 20 s, `RLIMIT_FSIZE` 0, `RLIMIT_CORE` 0, `RLIMIT_NOFILE` 64, `RLIMIT_NPROC` 0 (it starts no thread or process) and `PR_SET_NO_NEW_PRIVS` before the first byte is parsed. The parent kills it after 30 s, caps its stdout at 64 MiB and validates its JSON with pydantic. Page boxes ≤ 14,400 pt; render scale `min(200/72, sqrt(12 MP / (w·h)))`; JPEG 2000 header-first, refused above 4 MP. pypdf: 20 MB output caps, `page_tree_maximum_entries` 1,000, `jbig2dec_binary=None`, `strict=False`; XMP never reaches an XML parser (a bounded scan of ≤ 64 KiB that refuses `<!DOCTYPE` and `<!ENTITY`); no PDF JavaScript runs. *Stops:* memory and CPU exhaustion, decompression bombs, runaway pages, file writes, core dumps, new threads and processes (so no `exec`), privilege gain through setuid, the service token reaching the child, and a hostile child flooding the parent. *Does not stop:* code running inside the child from opening a socket on the vision network, reading what the vision user can read (the read-only models), or reading the parent's memory where the host allows same-user ptrace (Yama `ptrace_scope` 0; the docs ask operators to keep the distribution default of 1 or higher).
   - **The container.** Vision runs non-root, with a read-only root filesystem, `cap_drop: [ALL]`, `security_opt: no-new-privileges:true`, and Docker's default seccomp profile left on. *Stops:* privilege escalation, persistence and capability use. *Does not stop:* use of the vision process's own network.
   - **The network.** Vision needs no egress (its models are mounted read-only), so it moves to its own compose network, `vision`, with `internal: true`, shared only with the API and the worker, and leaves the default network: it cannot reach Postgres, Valkey, RustFS, Mailpit or the internet. *Stops:* a compromised vision process reaching the data stores, other services or the internet. *Does not stop:* reaching the API's HTTP port on that network (every sensitive route needs a credential the child does not hold), or a compromised vision returning false measurements (the worker checks their shape, not their truth).

   Tests: the hostile set, entity expansion, an external entity, a 10 MB XMP packet, the child's imports, and an `infra/tests` check that vision sits on the internal network only and cannot reach Postgres.
3. **Dependencies.** `pypdfium2==5.14.0`, `pypdf==6.19.0`; Stage 1 of 5B records whether PDFium build 8076 includes the CVE-2026-113022 fix (Chrome 156.0.8078.47) in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md`, and moves to a fixed build under decision 30. Banned in `test_dependencies.py`: PyMuPDF, pikepdf, poppler, Ghostscript, MuPDF, pdf.js, reportlab, fpdf2, borb.
4. **`POST /v1/render/document-page`** → a PNG ≤ 1,600 px through the guard (stored as `document-preview` by `storeServerFile`); **`POST /v1/convert/portrait`** → a PNG ≤ 640 px from JPEG, JP2 or J2K through the guard; `/v1/embed/face` with `subject=chip` decodes through the guard.
5. **Classification and extraction** (TypeScript, `packages/checks/src/document/`). Visible text-layer lines for born-digital PDFs, else OCR; a field whose text-layer value disagrees with the OCR of its box takes the OCR value, or answers `input_missing`. Lexicons per class for en, es, fr, de (anchor weight 3, medium 2, weak 1, at most 3 counted repeats per phrase), layout features, a softmax with temperature 1.0 over the five classes plus `other` at constant score θ = 2.0, `unknown` below `min-confidence` or a 0.15 margin. These constants are provisional and ours, fixed here, and must not be tuned on the generated set (H23). Extraction: labelled issue date (two different labelled issue dates give `input_missing`; a numeric date whose two readings disagree on the recency outcome gives `input_missing`), labelled printed expiry, issuer, name and address from the addressee block (C58). The detected language and script are recorded; a document in another language or script is `unknown`.
6. **Checks and cascade** (H22: one mistake fails one user-behaviour check).
   - Type: `document_type_not_accepted` only when one of the five classes wins outside the step's `documentTypes`, or `other` wins on a document positively identified as an identity document (an MRZ, an ID-card layout) in a covered language; every other `other` or `unknown` is `document_type_unknown` (review). Vision 413 `document_too_large`, `too_many_pages` `document_too_many_pages`, `pdf_encrypted` `document_password_protected`, other 415/422 `document_unreadable`. `confidence` is the top probability (uncalibrated).
   - When type detection did not establish an accepted class, properties, expiry, recency and the comparisons answer `input_missing`; a contract test: a wrong upload fails one check with one hint.
   - Properties: each configured property found. Expiry (user behaviour): a printed expiry or lease end date before today minus `grace-days` fails `document_expired`; `feature_absent` for an established class whose feature-table row has no expiry (`utility_bill`, `bank_statement`, `pay_stub`, `tax_document`) and for a lease that prints no end date (a periodic tenancy). Recency per class (V13): `lease` passes when its printed term or end date covers today, else `input_missing` without either; `tax_document` passes with an issue date within 18 months (the latest tax year; ours); every other class fails `document_too_old` beyond `max-age-days`; a date more than one day in the future is `input_missing`.
   - Fabrication (H, `beta`): `score = 1 − Π(1 − wᵢ·sᵢ)` with the weak cues' combined contribution capped at 0.45: weak (≤ 0.2 each) incremental revisions, a repaired xref, an editor producer, CreationDate earlier than the printed issue date, Info/XMP disagreement, EXIF editor software; medium (0.4–0.5) a different font inside an amount line, mixed subsets of one base font, text layer against OCR, double JPEG with ELA; strong (≥ 0.6) hidden or covered text over a field, edits after a signature, active content, copy-move. The failure's reasons are cues contributing at least 0.1; `components` in metadata; confidence margin-based, capped at 0.6. DocumentID ≠ InstanceID and ModDate after the printed date are not cues.
   - Name (required; user behaviour with category `fraud`, so a bill in a partner's or former name can be replaced, hint `document_name_mismatch`; V12) and address: against the inquiry's `fields`, else the inquiry's latest government ID extraction, else the account; none gives `no_reference_data`.
7. **Held-out evaluation.** `fixtures-5b` writes, separately from the classifier's author, a held-out set (synthetic documents in other layouts, and blank US federal forms W-2, 1099 and 1040, public-domain works fetched by `scripts/fetch_fixtures.py` with pinned SHA-256 and filled with synthetic values); `integration-5b` publishes the confusion matrix and the covered languages in `docs/checks/document.md`.

### C62. Government ID NFC on the server

1. **Package `@openverify/emrtd`** (pure, depends only on `@openverify/core`; F09). Parsers over `asn1js@3.1.0` (already locked; decision 37; a test asserts the lockfile holds one `asn1js` version) and `pkijs@3.4.1`, keeping original byte ranges; all cryptography through `node:crypto`. A bounded TLV pre-scan (depth ≤ 32, ≤ 20,000 nodes) runs before `asn1js` on every chip file. Key and curve bounds (P19): RSA 1,024–8,192 bits, exponent < 2³²; EC fields ≤ 521 bits; explicit domain parameters accepted only when they equal a curve on an allowlist (brainpool P-192 to P-512, NIST P-192 to P-521); each verification runs under a time budget. `parseDg1` returns the MRZ lines (the job calls `parseMrz`, exact text, no OCR corrections); `parseDg2` handles ISO/IEC 19794-5 and 39794-5.
2. **Integrity and revocation are separate** (H02, H15). `verifyPassiveAuthentication` returns `integrity` and `revocation`:
   - **established:** an anchor chosen by AKI→SKI (else issuer DN, accepted only when that anchor's key verifies the DSC), not withdrawn, valid now; the DSC signature verifies; the DSC profile holds (`cA` false, `digitalSignature`, not a master-list signer or link certificate; the CSCA `cA` true with `keyCertSign`); the SOD's `contentType`, `messageDigest` and signature over the received signed-attribute bytes verify; every uploaded data group the SOD lists hashes identically; the DG1 issuer agrees with the DSC and CSCA country;
   - **broken:** an anchor chosen by AKI→SKI verifies the DSC, the SOD parsed without a structural defect, and the SOD signature fails or an uploaded DG's hash differs;
   - **not established:** anything else (no anchor or only a DN match that does not verify, a withdrawn anchor, a profile defect, a country disagreement, a malformed SOD, an unsupported algorithm, an expired certificate, no DSC).
   - `revocation`: `revoked` (on a current CRL), `unrevoked`, `undetermined` (no current CRL; a CSCA naming no distribution point).
   `dataGroups` lists each DG as `identical`, `not_identical`, `missing_from_upload` (the signed SOD lists it, the client did not send it) or `not_listed`.
3. **Passive authentication answer.** Established and `unrevoked` (or `undetermined` under `best-effort`, flagged): `passed`. Broken: `failed` (`sod_signature_invalid`, `dg_hash_mismatch`). Revoked: `failed` (`dsc_revoked`). Not established, or `undetermined` under `strict`: `not_applicable` with its reason (review), never `failed`: a trust-store gap or an issuer defect is ours to resolve (ADR 0002). A `UTO` issuer in production fails `specimen_issuer`. BSI defect lists and ICAO deviation lists are not ingested in Phase 5 (decision 41), so a known defect ends in review.
4. **Data-group checks** run on parsed DGs whatever the revocation, and on integrity:
   - established: judged normally;
   - not established: a mismatch fails (the data contradict the document or the face), a match answers `integrity_not_established` (never `passed`);
   - broken: `integrity_not_established`.
5. **Chip challenge and submit.** `POST /verifications/:id/chip-challenges` → 201 `{ id, nonce (8 CSPRNG bytes as 16 hex characters), expires-at }`, SHA-256 stored, TTL 300 s; the 11th challenge cancels the verification (a retryable attempt). Submit `{ "chip": { "challenge-id", "nonce", "active-authentication-response"? (≤ 1 KiB), "device-report"? } }`, consumed by a conditional update. The submit guard parses the SOD (bounded) and requires every DG among 1, 2, 14 and 15 that it lists (409 `chip_data_required`); a wrong, used or expired challenge is one 409 `chip_challenge_invalid`. The device report (protocols attempted and their outcomes, read time) is shown to operators only, never evidence.
6. **Required checks and why** (P01, P02, H01, H16; owner decisions 33–35). A leaked DG1, DG2 and SOD set must not verify a different person, and a chip without AA must never fail for lacking it:
   - **selfie comparison, required:** DG2 (decoded only after its hash verified, through the guard, `subject=chip`) against the inquiry's latest selfie centre, whose liveness the selfie step requires; without a selfie in the inquiry, `no_reference_data` (review): chip data alone does not identify the presenter. This binding is what stops a different person.
   - **MRZ comparison, required:** DG1 against the latest government ID MRZ of the same document code and issuer (another document gives `no_reference_data`); OCR confusables are tolerated when the printed MRZ's check digits failed; `not_requested` (non-blocking) when the pinned flow has no government ID step. This ties the chip to the photographed booklet.
   - **repeat detection, advisory, new and ours** (decision 35, V01): a sighting is the SOD signature's blind index on a `chip_reads` row with `selfie_bound` true (its NFC verification's selfie comparison passed and it ended `passed` or `needs_review`) in an unredacted inquiry of another account (the current inquiry and its account's inquiries excluded, through `inquiries.account_id`). A sighting answers `not_applicable`/`chip_seen_on_other_account`, review-level, unless the template sets `sighting-outcome: fail`; metadata counts sightings. It is advisory, as `id_repeat_detection` is in the Phase 3 default template: one person on two accounts is an operator policy (every inquiry without a reference id gets a new account), and the selfie binding, not this check, stops a different person. A read whose selfie did not match is never a sighting. Evaluated only with integrity established. Tests: the same chip in two inquiries without a reference id is review-level when required and recorded when advisory; an earlier face-mismatched read is not a sighting.
   - **chip authentication, required:** AA over this challenge's nonce with the DG15 key (trusted only with integrity established, else `integrity_not_established`), RSA ISO/IEC 9796-2 or ECDSA plain: passes when it verifies; fails `active_authentication_failed` when a well-formed response does not; `chip_data_malformed` for a malformed response; `active_authentication_withheld` (review) when the SOD lists DG15 and the response is missing; for a chip whose verified SOD lists no DG15, `chip_auth_not_server_verifiable` (non-blocking), or `active_authentication_unavailable` (review) with `require-active-authentication`. Chip authentication and PACE-CAM transcripts are not transferable; a device claim stays in metadata as `device-reported`.
   - **passive authentication and expiry, required** (expiry on DG1).
   After integrity is established, the worker stores `chip_reads` (with `selfie_bound` set in the decision transaction) and the `chip-portrait` PNG.
7. **Trust store** (P05, P06). Nothing is committed or shipped. `trust-anchors/manifest.json` lists sources with the list URL, the publisher's root CSCA certificate URL and its SHA-256 (recorded by `api-nfc` from the publisher's page and cross-checked with its published fingerprint), the eContentType `2.23.136.1.1.2`, the signer EKU `2.23.136.1.1.3` and a terms note: `bsi-de` enabled; `npkd-nl`, `ita`, `che` disabled. `trust-anchors:sync [--source <id>] [--import <file> --root <pem>]` (wrapped by `scripts/fetch_trust_anchors.sh`; `selfTest()` first):
   - fetches through the outbound client (5 MiB CRLs, 16 MiB lists, 15 s, no redirects; "national" means the CDP host is not under `icao.int`, checked on the parsed URL; ICAO PKD CRLs only with `OPENVERIFY_NFC_ALLOW_ICAO_CRLS=true`, default false);
   - verifies each list against its pinned root (signer EKU and key usage, AKI to the root or a verified link, the signer's CRL, `messageDigest`, the signature over original bytes);
   - verifies every CRL's signature under its anchor and requires `thisUpdate ≤ now < nextUpdate` and a `cRLNumber` not below the stored one (refused and audited otherwise);
   - in one transaction under a per-source advisory lock: refuses a list whose `signingTime` is older than the source's last (rollback), upserts its anchors' memberships (`last_seen_at`), and sets `withdrawn_at` on every membership of that source absent from the new list; an anchor with no current membership in any enabled source is withdrawn, and PA answers `trust_anchor_withdrawn`;
   - imports a link certificate only after it verifies under a held anchor; writes `trust_anchor_syncs`.
   PA never fetches anything (a test: a DSC whose CDP is a URL causes no network call). The refresh job runs only with `OPENVERIFY_TRUST_ANCHOR_REFRESH=true`. The sync never runs in CI.
8. **Sandbox anchor** (P17). The repository function that loads anchors for PA takes a mandatory `environmentKind` and adds `sandbox_only = false` in SQL for production; `emrtd` receives an already filtered trust object. `seed:dev` (`apps/api/src/seed/dev-seed.ts`) refuses when the resolved mode is `production` (`OPENVERIFY_MODE`, else `NODE_ENV`) and inserts the deterministic Utopia test CSCA, valid around now, with a CRL whose `nextUpdate` is now + 90 days (re-running the seed refreshes it). Tests (V04): a UTO chip in a production environment fails `specimen_issuer` (the specimen rule runs first, C62.3); a repository test shows that the production anchor loader returns no `sandbox_only` row after `seed:dev` on that database.
9. **Self-test and runtime.** `selfTest()` (explicit-parameter brainpool keys) runs as `emrtd`'s first test, at worker start and at the start of the sync; on failure PA answers `algorithm_unsupported`. CI's Node is pinned to 22.22.1, the image's build (F22).
10. **Console API.** `GET /api/v1/dashboard/trust-anchors` (`settings:read`): sources, enabled, last sync, the list's `signingTime` and SHA-256, anchors per country, withdrawn anchors, CRL freshness, the sandbox anchor.

## Rules for every agent

- **TDD:** RED first, seen failing for the intended reason; the RED commit compiles and typechecks; GREEN; refactor on green. Evidence in `docs/tdd/phase5-<agent>.tdd.md`.
- **Shared files:** builders never edit the C54 list; route schemas, the OpenAPI snapshot, `@openverify/node` and API fixtures change only through the integration agent; a missing hook or schema change is a `contractDeviation`.
- **Shared resources:** commits are path-scoped under `flock /tmp/openverify-git.lock` after `git diff --cached --quiet`; full-stack e2e under `flock /tmp/openverify-e2e.lock`; long gates in the foreground with a 600,000 ms timeout; flaky tests made deterministic, never retried.
- **No network in tests:** `MockAgent` or local stubs from our own synthetic responses; injected resolvers; no real registry, RDAP, Twilio, RPC or trust-store call in CI.
- **Honesty (ADR 0002):** no `passed` on an error, without input or from an unbound provider; a gap on our side ends in review, never fraud; H-tier results carry a confidence; every default is provisional with a source; no fixture special case beyond the documented sandbox rules (Utopia, reserved domains, the test-capture rule).
- **PII:** none in logs (except under `pii`), metadata, problems, events or git; synthetic data only.
- **Licences and versions:** rule 4; versions at least 14 days old unless an owner decision says otherwise; no new build script without an `allowBuilds` decision; downloads only through `scripts/fetch_*` with pinned SHA-256 (trust anchors: pinned roots).
- **Content:** no Persona name, branding or copied text in product UI, docs or messages.

### `checks-contact-crypto` (C55.8, C55.9, C56, C57)

1. **Phone destinations (C55.8).** Only international form is read (`+`, a digit, then digits, spaces, hyphens, dots and parentheses), at most 32 characters and 15 digits, surrounding whitespace trimmed; letters, extensions and `tel:` are refused. A valid non-geographic number (`+800`, `+882`) answers `otp_destination_not_allowed` (refusal `no_region`): it is valid, but no allowed country holds it.
2. **Email destinations (C55.8).** The local part is an RFC 5321 dot-string over the WHATWG atext set (no leading, trailing or doubled dots). `url.domainToASCII` runs only on a domain of ASCII letters, digits, hyphens and dots, or another script, because it percent-decodes (`%2e`) and parses IPv4 forms (`0x7f.1`); a domain whose last label is numeric is `domain_ip_literal`. Surrounding ASCII whitespace is trimmed.
3. **Masked destinations (C52.2).** Phone: the international format with the calling code and the last 4 digits (`+1 ••• ••• 0100`). Email: the first local character, `•••@`, and the ASCII domain, shortened from the left with `…` to 64 characters.
4. **Organization name (C55.9), stricter than written.** After NFKC, controls and line or paragraph separators become spaces; format, surrogate, private-use and unassigned characters go; every word that is a URL (`scheme://`, `www.`) or a host name (labels with an alphabetic TLD of 2+ letters) is dropped, because SMS apps link bare domains; `@`, `#` and every run of 4 or more `\p{Nd}` digits go; the result is cut to 40 code points.
5. **OTP comparison (C56).** Input `OtpCheckState { confirmed, sends, wrongEntries, maxWrongEntries, limitedReason }` (`maxWrongEntries` passed by the job, since checks cannot import contracts). Order: passed (confirmed, with a destination); `otp_attempts_exhausted`; `otp_delivery_unavailable` (any limited reason, even before a destination was stored); `input_missing` (no state, destination or accepted send); else `otp_not_confirmed`. Metadata `{ channel, sends, 'failed-attempts', 'limited-reason'? }`.
6. **Disposable (C56).** The most specific domain level wins; at each level the operator's `deny-domains` and `allow-domains` are read before the file, and an operator match decides without the file; with neither the answer is `list_unavailable`. The file is recorded as a provenance `models` entry `{ id: 'disposable-email-domains@6fcca80', sha256 }`. Metadata `{ match, 'match-depth' }`; a one-label TLD in the list is never matched.
7. **MX (C56).** Input `MxLookupResult` (`mx`, `no_mx` with address counts, `not_found`, `error`, `not_configured`). A usable exchange passes even beside a null MX; only null MX records fail `domain_rejects_mail`; an `mx` answer with no record is `execution_error`; no lookup at all is `provider_not_configured`, never a pass. Metadata `{ route, 'record-count'? }`.
8. **Wire encodings (C57.3).** `signature`: `0x` hex for EVM; hex (optional `0x`) for TRON; base64 of 65 bytes for BIP-137 and BIP-322 legacy; `smp` plus base64 of the witness, or the base64 alone, for BIP-322 simple; base58 or padded base64 of 64 bytes for Solana. `signed-message` is the padded base64 of the exact bytes signed (a Solana off-chain envelope); without it the challenge's UTF-8 bytes are taken as signed. A scheme the chain does not take is `signature_invalid`; without a scheme it is inferred (by family; BIP-137 for Litecoin and Dogecoin; for Bitcoin, the prefix or a 65-byte header 27–42).
9. **Challenge messages (C57.2).** EVM: EIP-4361 with the EIP-55 address, `URI` the origin's normalised href, a scheme before the domain only when not https. Solana: Sign-In with Solana, `Chain ID: mainnet`. Bitcoin, testnet, Litecoin, Dogecoin, TRON, XRP Ledger: the CAIP-122 header `<host> wants you to sign in with your <chain name> account:` and the full CAIP-2 id. Fields in order: Version 1, Nonce, Issued At, Expiration Time, Request ID. Statement `<org> asks you to prove you control this wallet. Signing is free and moves no funds.` (English only; the name folded to the EIP-4361 statement set), or `Prove you control this wallet. …` without a name.
10. **BIP-322 simple (C57.4).** Low s is required whatever `allow-high-s` says (a BIP-322 rule); `allow-high-s` governs EIP-191, BIP-137 and TRON. Strict DER; SIGHASH_ALL for P2WPKH with exactly the signature and a 33-byte key; SIGHASH_DEFAULT (64 bytes) or 65 bytes ending in ALL for P2TR. A script-path spend, an annex, P2PKH, P2SH, P2WSH, witness v2–v16 and the `ful` and `pof` formats are `signature_inconclusive`.
11. **BIP-137 (C57.4).** `loose` takes any header and derives the claimed address's own script type from the recovered key (flagged `bip137-loose`); `strict` needs the address's header range (else `signature_invalid`). A P2SH address whose key's P2SH-P2WPKH does not match is `signature_inconclusive`, never `signature_address_mismatch`. BIP-322 legacy is BIP-137 restricted to P2PKH.
12. **EVM contract wallets (C57.4).** `cryptoWalletContractCheck(input, template)` gives the job `{ address, digest, signature }` to ask the `evmRpc`; the answer is `ContractWalletResult { hasCode, signatureValid: boolean | null }`. No code: another key's 65-byte signature fails `signature_address_mismatch`, other bytes `signature_invalid`. Code: `null` is `execution_error`, false `signature_invalid`, true passes with `allow-contract-wallets` or an equal expected address, else `contract_wallet_not_allowed`. An ERC-6492 envelope is `signature_inconclusive`.
13. **Statement (C57.4).** It also passes when a contract wallet's ERC-1271 answer accepted the bytes. Solana off-chain envelopes v0 and v1 are unwrapped (v1 signatories ascending and unique; v0 lengths must agree); the claimed key must be a signatory; metadata `envelope`.
14. **Validity (C57.1).** TRON starts with `T`, XRP Ledger with `r`; segwit v2–v16 (`witness_unknown`) are valid addresses with inconclusive ownership; Litecoin `ltc1p` is accepted; an unknown chain is `input_missing`; BIP-173's v1+ vectors are `address_invalid`, BIP-350's wrong-variant addresses `address_checksum_invalid`. `require-evm-checksum` also applies in the evaluator. Metadata adds `address-type` and, for Solana, `on-curve` (the golden fixture was corrected in Stage 4). The disallowed-chain check records `allowed-chains` as its threshold.
15. **Code layout.** `packages/checks/src/contact/phase5-config.ts` (`phase5CheckConfig`, `phase5ResultOptions`, `booleanIn`, `stringListIn`, `enabledChecksOf`) is shared with the crypto evaluators and not exported from the package index. The evaluator-against-contract reason tests live in `apps/api` (`jobs/contact-contract.test.ts`, `jobs/crypto-wallet-contract.test.ts`), since checks cannot import contracts. The vectors' licence rows were added in Stage 4 (`THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md` §4.4).

### `checks-matching` (C58, C59)

1. **Flags (C58.1, C58.2).** Match flags are kebab-case (`script-mismatch`, not C58.2's `script_mismatch`; also `swapped`, `middle-name-mismatch`, `suffix-mismatch`, `precision-limited`, `approximate`, `transposed`, `near-match`, `century-inferred`, `unreadable`, `legal-form-mismatch`, `city-mismatch`, `subdivision-mismatch`, `unit-one-sided`, `house-number-one-sided`, `street-missing`, `po-box-one-sided`, `too-many-fuzzy-components`, `family-name-missing`, `given-name-missing`, `family-name-initial`, `name-incomplete`, `not-accepted`), as the metadata keys and the C57.4 and C61 flags are. Methods are reported only on full and partial results.
2. **Names (C58.2).** Initials agree only for given and middle names. A family name given as one initial, or a name without its given or family part on one side, is `missing`; a contradiction in the part present is still `none`. Different middle names cap the level at partial; a one-sided middle name does not lower `full`. A title the nickname table also lists as a given name (Don) is not stripped. In one-string names, one token against several is `missing` (`name-incomplete`). The generational suffixes ii, iii and iv are ours.
3. **Nicknames (C58.3).** 1,390 pairs: 1,341 authored and 130 from 132 reviewed Wikidata rows (labels mostly `mul`, so each pair's language was assigned in review). The raw query result is not committed; `nicknames-wikidata.tsv` records the endpoint, date, result SHA-256 and counts, and `nicknames.sparql` the query. Non-Latin candidates were not reviewed.
4. **Addresses (C58.4).** `none`: a different unit (a neighbour) or a different country. `missing`: a one-sided house number, a different city without a postal code (`city-mismatch`; with the same postal code it is partial), two fuzzy components, or a PO box against a street. A country absent on one side is inherited. A differing subdivision caps the level at partial. PO boxes compare by number. Separable German and Dutch street suffixes are split only for AT, BE, CH, DE, LI, LU and NL. Ours: the house-number order by country, the floor designators, the house-number markers and the English ordinal words.
5. **Data and CI (C58.4).** 48 sources were added to `data/manifest.json` (46 libpostal files, OpenCage `state_codes.yaml`, libaddressinput `countryinfo.txt`); the CI e2e job fetches every manifest entry, so they are cached with the disposable list but are not in the apps image. Three upstream postal-code examples contradict their own patterns (BY 20050, EE 1001, GB RH6 OHP) and are left out, recorded in the builder and the NOTICE. libpostal lists `saint|st` among personal titles, so street and city words read titles' canonical forms too.
6. **Business names (C58.6).** `&` and `+` read as "and"; legal forms come from libpostal's company types, initials written apart included (G.m.b.H.); one name's words all found in the other is partial, with one-word names of at least three letters.
7. **C58.7.** The Jaro-Winkler vector test is a characterization of the existing `name-matching.ts` against both oracles (jellyfish 1.2.1, RapidFuzz 3.14.6, run once offline through a PEP 723 script, no lockfile change). Extra caps: dates 64, postal codes 20, identifiers 254 code points.
8. **Database checks (C59.3).** A precision-limited birth date refused by `accept-precision-limited-birthdate: false`, or a transposed one not accepted, counts as `missing` (so `insufficient_data`, never unmet); identity metadata names the group fields the step did not collect (`not-requested-fields`). Per-field checks answer `not_requested`, then `input_missing`, then `no_matching_record`, then `no_reference_data`. The birth-date check answers `birthdate_ambiguous` for any transposition. The inquiry comparison answers `input_missing` (no values or no inquiry fields passed), `no_reference_data`, a failure on any mismatch, else a pass. A name is collected by `name_first`/`name_last` and an address by `address_street_1` (`MATCH_FIELD_STEP_FIELDS`).
9. **AAMVA checks (C59.5).** A no-match without its field list is `record_mismatch` for the identity check when no field is ignored, else `execution_error` (an ignored field may be all that differs); `execution_error` for the name and number checks. When every mismatched field is ignored the identity check passes, listing them in `ignored-mismatches`. Provider field names are kept only if they look like field names. `aamvaSkipsVerification` is the pure skip decision; `aamvaEligible` belongs to `api-database`.
10. **Naming.** The database config helpers stay internal (a package export would clash with the contact helpers); `isStandInResult` is named apart from the government ID folder's `isCheckResult`. No new reason and no config change were needed.

4. **Guess budget (C55.5).** A GCRA of 20 a day per destination and channel: the 21st wrong code trips it (429, recorded as `guess`), and a cache marker (`openverify:otp-guess:<index>`) keeps sends and confirms to that destination refused until the rule would allow one again.
5. **Codes (C55.5).** The fifth wrong try answers 422 `otp_incorrect` with `attempts-remaining: 0` and closes the code `exhausted`; later tries 409 `otp_code_exhausted`. A code replaced while a confirm was in flight reads as `otp_expired`. A send after the fifth submits the verification even while a code can still be entered.
6. **Problems.** A client submit of an initiated phone or email verification answers 409 `otp_not_confirmed` with its own detail; the 503 of `otp_rate_limited` has its own detail; `otp_resend_too_soon` also carries `Retry-After`; the frozen sink schema has no 422, so a number that is not E.164 once separators are removed finds nothing (`{ data: [] }`).
7. **Closing codes.** A canceled or submitted verification's open code is closed as `superseded` (C53.2 has no closed status). An email code's provider is `smtp` in every environment.
8. **MX cache (C53.5).** Cached and returned MX answers drop the exchange host names (a null MX reads `''`, any other `exchange`); keys are `openverify:mx:<sha256(domain)>`; positive 1 hour, negative 5 minutes; failures are never cached.
9. **Disposable list.** `DISPOSABLE_LIST_PIN` holds the manifest's id and SHA-256 (a test keeps them equal); a file of another digest, or none, gives no list. The default path is `<repo or image root>/data/disposable_email_blocklist.conf`.
10. **Budget trip record (C55.7).** One audit entry an hour, decided by comparing the newest entry's `created_at` (database time) with the application clock under a transaction advisory lock; a failed write is logged with ids and the error name, and the 429 stands.

### `vision-document` (C60.1–C60.4)

1. **The child (C60.2).** The parent runs `python -I -B -m openverify_vision.guarded.worker`: `-B` because `RLIMIT_FSIZE` is 0 and `-I` ignores `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE`. The child reads the parent's request header (at most 64 KiB of JSON: task, parameters, limits, document length) before it clears its environment and sets `PR_SET_NO_NEW_PRIVS` and the limits, and reads the document only after hardening. The limits travel in that header (`document_analysis.GUARD_LIMITS`). The `probe` task is the guard's self-test and never reads a document.
2. **OCR on born-digital pages (C60.1).** On a page with visible text-layer lines, `ocr` is the recognition of each visible line's own box, padded by 0.3 of its height: one reading per line, empty readings dropped, their confidence kept however low. No whole-page detection runs on such a page, so text drawn only as an image there is not read. A page without a visible text layer, and a photo, are read whole. Reason: whole-page detection plus recognition exceeded the 4 s budget for a 3-page bill; the box reading is exactly the "OCR of its box" C60.5 compares a field with.
3. **`text_layer_ocr_agreement`:** the mean over visible lines of difflib's similarity between the line and its box's reading (both NFKC-casefolded, whitespace removed), weighted by length; a box read as nothing scores 0; `null` without a visible line or when OCR read nothing (also without the text models).
4. **Rasters** are grey (one byte a pixel), so four 12 MP pages fit the 64 MiB output cap.
5. **Hidden reasons as built.** Render modes 3 and 7 are `render_mode_invisible`; a clip is read as each clip path's bounding box; `covered` is at least 90% of the character box under an opaque fill, image or shading drawn after it; `background_fill` is within 8 levels of the topmost opaque fill behind the character's centre (white without one), or a fill alpha under 8; text over an image or shading is never judged by colour; `too_small` is a loose box under 2 pt. Not detected (left to the agreement): text clips (render modes 4–7) and annotations drawn over text.
6. **Lines.** Characters run into lines in content order and break on a visibility change, a baseline change, going backwards, or a gap over 2 line heights; `font_size_pt` is the line's most common PDFium size; lines sort top to bottom, at most 600, each cut to 300 UTF-16 units.
7. **`pdf` measurements as built.** `annotations` on the analysed pages only; `form_fields` from the AcroForm's terminal fields through pypdf (bounded walk), else the widgets on the analysed pages; `actions.javascript` is the larger of the raw `/JS` names (outside strings, comments and streams, escapes decoded, object streams included) and PDFium's document JavaScript count; `embedded_files` the larger of the raw names and PDFium's attachments; `rich_media` counts `/RichMedia` and `/RichMediaExecute`; `launch`, `submit_form`, `goto_remote` by name; a GoTo open action counts nothing; `revisions` is the `startxref` sections minus one for a linearized file, at least 1; `bytes_after_last_eof` ignores surrounding whitespace; `xmp` is also `null` when refused or encrypted; `model_versions.pdf_parser` is `null` when pypdf cannot read the document.
8. **`edits_after_signature`:** increments after the earliest signature's byte-range end, each the objects up to the next `%%EOF` (object streams decoded); excluded when it holds a DSS and only catalog, DSS, VRI, data streams and structure, or a DocTimeStamp and only catalog, signature fields, AcroForm, zero-area form XObjects and pages whose contents name nothing new. A later regular signature's increment counts as an edit, as the plan words it (leases signed by two parties show it; an owner decision may exclude it).
9. **`ela_text_cell_score`:** JPEG only; the largest gap over the re-save ladder between one OCR line's mean normalised JPEG-ghost curve and the median of the others, in a window of at most 2,048 px on the JPEG grid; `null` under 4 lines with varying cells. A paste off the 8×8 grid is not seen by it.
10. **Chip portraits (C60.4).** The 4 MP header-first bound applies to every chip portrait (JPEG included), and a header past Pillow's own bomb bound is also 413; another format 415, an undecodable one 422. With `subject=chip`, `/v1/embed/face` embeds the largest face, as for a selfie.
11. **Problems.** A page beyond the document (render) and a corrupt photo are 422 without a code; `503 guarded_worker_failed` carries no `Retry-After`; `ProblemError` gained an optional `code`.
12. **Tests changed:** `services/vision/tests/test_app_document.py` (the 501 stub cases replaced by the built routes). `ocr/recognition.recognize` gained an optional `batch_size` (default unchanged). No hook, route schema, golden fixture or API file changed.

### `checks-5b` (C60.5, C60.6, C61.2–C61.7)

1. **Lexicons (C60.5).** OpenVerify's own phrase lists per covered language (`packages/checks/src/document/lexicons/{en,es,fr,de}.ts`): class cues (anchor, medium, weak, with the classes each supports), field labels (`issueDate`, `period`, `genericDate`, `expiry`, `leaseStart`, `leaseTerm`, `taxYear`, `name`, `otherDate`), identity labels, function words, range words, duration units, joint words, pay-column headings and signature words. Language-neutral cues (kWh, MWh, m³, therms, ccf, IBAN, BIC, SWIFT) count for whichever language the document is in and never tell the language.
2. **Provisional constants of our own** (fixed in the code, never tuned on the generated set, H23): a word of 5 letters or more reads as a lexicon word at Jaro-Winkler 0.93 with the same first letter; the language is the covered one with the most distinct entries, at least 2, ties to the first of en, es, fr, de; an identity document is an MRZ-like line (28–44 of A–Z, 0–9 and `<`, holding `<<`) or at least 3 identity labels of one language; layout feature weights are 2 for a feature of one class and 1 for a shared one (money column, transaction table, valid IBAN, earnings and deductions, numbered boxes, contract layout, meter readings); the issuer is the tallest line in the top quarter of page 1 at least 1.2 times the median line height, else a printed web or email domain; a label's value is on its own line, on the same row to its right, or on the line below within 1.5 line heights; a text-layer line agrees with its OCR at Jaro-Winkler 0.9 (dates by their readings).
3. **Date orders:** English reads month first for US and PH, both orders for CA or no country, day first otherwise; Spanish both orders for US, day first otherwise; French and German day first; a two-digit year is 20yy; only the reference country (inquiry, then ID, then account) sets the order, never a country guessed from a postal code.

Two values within one rank conflict. A rank whose value OCR contradicts yields no issue date and no fallback. The properties check counts an issue date found when printed, even conflicting, ambiguous or contradicted, so recency reviews it instead of a retry hint.
5. **Type check:** a document with no letter read fails `document_unreadable` (the user can act on it). With integer weights, T 1.0 and θ 2.0 the 0.15 margin rule binds only when `min-confidence` is set below 0.5.
6. **Recency per class (V13):** a lease passes while its end covers today; an ended term, a start more than a day ahead, only a start, or two different ends answer `input_missing`; an ended lease is failed by the expiry check only (H22). The 18-month tax window is recorded as threshold `max-age-months` 18 (provisional). A lease without an end answers `feature_absent` with flag `periodic-tenancy`.
7. **Fabrication corrections:** a repaired xref is `pdf_incremental_update` (component `xref-repaired`); an embedded file alone is no active content (e-invoices embed XML); hidden text counts only on born-digital pages, for covered, background-coloured or render-mode-3 lines holding a digit; `text-layer-mismatch` is 1 when an extracted field's text layer disagreed with OCR, else (0.8 − the least page agreement) / 0.2 clamped to [0, 1]; fabrication is not gated by the type check; it passes only with a medium or strong cue measured, else `input_missing`; a single medium cue (weight ≤ 0.5) cannot fail the default 0.5 alone.
8. **Comparisons:** a joint addressee matches on either holder, a lone given name taking the last holder's family name; an undecidable comparison is `input_missing`; `postal_code_mismatch` only when the postal code is the sole contradiction.
9. **Registry reading (C61.1, C61.7):** answers are read in the order of asking and the first record found decides; without a record, any failed register gives `execution_error`; otherwise an authoritative `not_found` with an entered number gives `registration_number_not_found`, any other miss `no_registry_record`; a country no bound register covers gives identity `no_registry_record` and service `provider_not_configured` (flag `country-not-covered`); service available passes when any covering register answered.

### `emrtd` (C62.1–C62.4, C62.6, C62.9, C65.3)

1. **`PassiveAuthenticationOptions`** gains a required `issuerCountry: (icaoCode) => alpha2 | null` (the ICAO table lives in `@openverify/checks`; callers pass `nfcIssuerCountry`: UTO gives UT) and an optional `selfTest` (else PA runs `selfTest()` once per process). 2. **`INTEGRITY_GAPS`** gains `input_missing` (DG1 not uploaded), already in the frozen PA row.
3. **A DN-only anchor** (no matching AKI) whose key verifies the DSC never makes integrity `broken`: a failing SOD signature or DG hash under it is `not_established` with `trust_anchor_unavailable` and the flags `issuer-dn-match` with `sod-signature-invalid` or `dg-hash-mismatch` (H02).
4. **Precedence:** `broken` takes precedence over anchor or DSC expiry, withdrawal, profile and country gaps; a failed self-test overrides everything, `broken` included, with `algorithm_unsupported`.
5. **SOD structure:** a `messageDigest` differing from the content's hash is `sod_signature_invalid` (break-capable); a signed content type other than the LDS OID, or no signed attributes, is `chip_data_malformed`; a scheme OpenSSL refuses under the key (PKCS#1 v1.5 under a PSS-only key) is `algorithm_unsupported`, never invalid; a signer identifier naming no embedded certificate falls back to the embedded certificate whose key verifies the SOD (flag `signer-identifier-mismatch`), else `dsc_missing`.
6. **Revocation:** `no_distribution_point` only when neither the chosen CSCA nor the DSC names a distribution point, else `crl_unavailable`; CRLs match by the anchor's country and must be current; a CRL without `nextUpdate` or unreadable counts as absent; PA does not re-verify a stored CRL's signature (the sync does) and fetches nothing.
7. **Hashes** are computed over a data group's TLV extent; with padding after it the hash over the whole upload is also accepted (flag `trailing-bytes`); bytes after the SOD's TLV are tolerated and flagged.
8. **`dsc_revoked`** fails only when an anchor verified the DSC; a broken and revoked chip fails with both reasons, the break first. The specimen rule reads DG1's issuer, the DSC's and the anchor's country.
9. **MRZ comparison (C62.6):** the latest reading of the same issuer (`issuing-authority-code`) and document category (P, V, else card; an unknown class matches); because the issuer selects the reading, `issuer_mismatch` is never emitted (it stays in the table). 10. **Selfie comparison:** `input_missing` for a portrait without a face or never decoded while DG2's integrity holds; `integrity_not_established` when DG2's hash did not verify; `execution_error` for a vision or selfie embedding failure.
11. **Expiry** is judged on DG1's integrity: an expired document fails even unverified, a current one is evidence only with integrity established. `expired_within_grace_period` is a reason on a passed result; Stage 4 added it to `NFC_EVALUATOR_OUTCOMES.passed` (see Stage 4).
12. **Device claims** are metadata `device-reported`, only names from `DEVICE_REPORTED_PROTOCOLS`, deduplicated.
13. **Master-list caps** are `MASTER_LIST_LIMITS` (16 MiB, depth 64, 1,000,000 TLVs), apart from the chip caps; chip ASN.1 values are capped at 2 MiB.
14. **The self-test** runs as the emrtd suite's Vitest `globalSetup` and stops the suite on a platform without explicit brainpool.
15. **Fixtures (C65.3):** the Utopia CSCA is valid from 1 January of the previous UTC year for 16 years; the seed's CRL number is the day number, so the sync's monotonic rule holds across re-seeds; the 16,384-bit modulus is a key spec (`oversized-rsa`), the non-allowlisted explicit curve is secp256k1; `TEST_CHIP_VARIANTS` gained five variants and `buildChip` gained options.

### Checks and their evidence

1. **C62.2, revocation by the CSCA's name (P001, P007).** This amends `emrtd` Stage 2 amendment 6 ("CRLs match by the anchor's country"). Passive authentication judges revocation only on current CRLs of the CSCA's country that were also issued under the CSCA's name: the chosen anchor's subject DN, else the DSC's issuer DN. Serial numbers are unique per issuer only (RFC 5280 §4.1.2.2), so another CSCA of the same country can neither revoke the signer nor vouch for it. A CRL that a rolled-over key signs under the same name still counts. The issuer key id is deliberately not the scope: after a rollover, it would send every DSC of the old key to review.
2. **C60.5, printed dates (P006).** `parseVizDate` refuses a day past 31 and answers `null`; it used to throw `RangeError`, failing the document job on every attempt. 21. **C60.1, the OCR bounded by work (P009).** A document's recognition may cost at most 2,400 line units, a line's width in line heights (clamped to 320/48..64).
   - The budget is shared equally among the analysed pages still to read; a photo gets all of it. Each page's lines are read in order until its share is spent.
   - A line left unread has no OCR reading and is left out of `text_layer_ocr_agreement`. Not measured is not the same as read as nothing, which still scores 0. So `ocr` holds at most one reading per visible line read.
   - The OCR stops 40 s after the analysis began and answers 503 `guarded_worker_failed`.
   - The document benchmark gains `--sample dense` (4 analysed pages of 600 visible lines) with a 10 s budget, logged in CI and never gated.
22. **C60.1's errors and vision-document amendment 11 (P003).** A PDF that stops the analysis' guarded child on its own limits is refused 422 `pdf_unprocessable`, because a retry would hit the same limit. Those limits are SIGXCPU; SIGABRT, SIGSEGV or SIGBUS; Python out of address space (now exit status 3); and the 64 MiB output cap.
   - 503 `guarded_worker_failed` remains for:
     - a failed spawn, the 30 s kill timer or SIGKILL;
     - the child's own errors and malformed output;
     - render and portrait children past their limits;
     - the OCR deadline.
   - This replaces C60.1's "503 when the worker was killed or timed out" for the analysis. The API needs no change: it already maps 422 `pdf_unprocessable` to `document_unreadable`.
23. **C60.1, unreadable scans (P012, P017).** A PDF the raw scanner cannot read to its end is refused 422 `pdf_unprocessable`. That covers:
   - its 3,000,000-token budget (comments and stray bytes now count);
   - its 200,000 kept objects and an object stream's budget;
   - a stream without `endstream`;
   - an object-stream header with an integer of more than 18 digits.

   Elsewhere such an integer is kept as no value. Any scanner exception is a refusal, never a crash.
24. **C60.1 and vision-document amendment 5, page complexity (P013, P014).** A page is refused 422 `pdf_unprocessable` when:

   - a form nested deeper than 8 levels draws anything;

   Each character is now tested only against the opaque objects indexed over its centre.
25. **Vision-document amendment 5, masks and form clips (P015, P016).**

26. **C60.4 and vision-document amendment 8, owner decision 5 (K11).** `edits_after_signature` excludes an increment that adds only a later signature. The increment qualifies only if all of these hold:
