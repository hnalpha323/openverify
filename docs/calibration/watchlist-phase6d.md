# Watchlist matching: the Workflow 6D calibration (C78.7; owner decisions 6, 7 and 8 after 6C)

This report measures the watchlist matcher and the re-screen at Workflow 6D's base and at the head
of 6D's Stage 1, on one evaluation seed. It decides nothing about quality: in 6D no C78.4 constant,
rule, threshold, preset or cap changed, so it checks that every created match is identical
(owner decision 8 after Workflow 6C), measures performance against the budgets of owner decision 1
after 6C, measures the share of each switchable change, and gives a verdict for each change and
each budget.

Every number here comes from synthetic data. **Synthetic variation is not real-world variation**:
the names, name frequencies, typos and customer populations are generated by rules, and real lists,
customers and data entry differ from them in ways no generator captures.

The calibration changes no matcher or re-screen code, constant, threshold, preset, cap or
generator law. It runs them. A benchmark step that cannot run because of a defect is fixed
test-first in `packages/watchlist/scripts/benchmark-*` only, never changing what a metric means,
and listed under "Amendments".

## Method

This section and the decision rules below were written and committed before the evaluation seed
was drawn. A change made afterwards is listed under "Amendments", with its reason.

### What is compared

- **Base:** Workflow 6D's launch commit, `9882aee37bc31a8d49b1a6664032e6aa074b0fc1` (Workflow 6C's
  head as pushed).
- **Head:** the head of Workflow 6D's Stage 1, `60483a3b` (every Stage 1 commit of `matcher-6d`,
  `rescreen-6d` and `platform-6d`). The head worktree is pinned to the commit that adds this method;
  between `60483a3b` and it only `docs/` and `packages/watchlist/scripts/benchmark-*` change, so it
  runs Stage 1's code.
- **One benchmark at both trees.** The base carries the head worktree's
  `packages/watchlist/scripts/benchmark-*` and `packages/watchlist/src/testing/heavy-headed.ts`,
  overlaid uncommitted, so both trees run the same steps and draw the same tables.
- **Two worktrees** under `/tmp` (`/tmp/cal6d-base`, `/tmp/cal6d-head`), never the main tree. Each
  is prepared with `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile --offline`, the `data/` files copied from the
  main tree except `variant-groups.json`, and its own shipped variant groups built with its own
  script (`pnpm -s build:variant-groups`). Their SHA-256 is recorded under "Seed and data".
- **One seed** for both trees, so every difference is paired.
- **Namespaces** (each tree's containers): `c6d-base`, `c6d-head`, and `-r2`, `-r3` for the second
  and third re-screen databases; never reused.

### Independence (H21, V12)

- The seed is drawn after this method is committed, with `openssl rand -hex 16`. Nobody knew the
  held-out groups, the population or the tables before it existed; the Stage 1 agents measured on
  development tables of their own seeds and never read the held-out folder.
- The 6B set is screened against each tree's own hold-out build of that seed, and the benchmark
  asserts before any screening that no group a held-out case draws on survives in it.
- Nothing generated is committed. Lists, cases, customers, builds, observations and every
  per-query result stay under `/tmp/openverify-eval-6d/`. Only aggregates are published here.

### The sets and tables

- **The 6B held-out set**, unchanged: 2,000 listed persons, 600 listed companies, 500 held-out-group
  cases for each of the two group sources and 10,000 person customers, in its own list of about
  3,600 entries (the 2,600 listed persons and companies and the group cases' entries), screened by
  name only and with the listed birth date.
- **The scale population** (owner decision 5 after 6B, `SCALE_SIZES`): 1,050 listed persons, 400
  listed companies, 1,500 person customers and 500 company customers, embedded in the 100,000- and
  2,000,000-name performance tables (3,450 queries per scale).
- **The performance tables** (C78.7), from the shared module `src/testing/heavy-headed.ts` (owner
  decision 6 after 6C): Zipf's law with exponent 1 over the raw groups' Latin forms and 40,000
  fictional names; the sanctions profile at 100,000 names (the C78.7 gate's table) and the PEP
  profile at 2,000,000, imported through the sync in 1 and 8 parts. The subjects are 150 listed
  entries' primary names with their birth dates and 150 unlisted names from the same law.
- **The re-screen database:** the 100,000-name table with 200,000 recurring `watchlist` reports in
  one sandbox environment, then syncs adding 100, 300 and 1,000 entries.

### What is measured

1. **The identity check** (decision 8), keyed by query and preset, through
   `benchmark:quality --step identity`: the kept entry ids, `fixed` (the matches to a scale
   table's own entries), the coverage `limits`, the `errors` and the SHA-256 of the full created
   matches (every member a run returns: the entry key, the score, the content and risk-data
   hashes, the matched name, and the snapshot's entity with its alias type, the match types, the
   qualifiers and the components with the list name, name level, methods, preset and minimum).
   It covers the 6B set (both query variants and the customers, at `exact`, `strict` and
   `tolerant`) and the scale population at both scales. `benchmark:performance --step identity`
   compares the first measure round of both performance tables subject by subject as well (the
   kept count, the listed subject found, the candidates, truncation, the cap, the reach, the
   errors and the digest).
2. **The executed runs** (decision 8, review C004 at scale). After each `rescreen-<n>` on the first
   re-screen database of each tree, `rescreen-runs --size <n>` executes every `list_update` run the
   re-screen inserted, with its trigger sync and its report's pinned configuration, nothing
   persisted. Each must keep a match on an entry the sync changed. At 1,000 entries it runs after
   `rescreen-check --size 1000`, and every run report whose full screening keeps such matches must
   keep at least as many. Each tree's runs are digested by their subjects, so the two trees' run
   sets can be compared.
3. **C78.7**, the screening p95 of the `watchlist` job at `tolerant` with the shipped groups, at both
   scales and both trees: three takes each, a take being one `<scale>-measure --rounds 1` (20
   warm-ups, then the 300 subjects). The base's and the head's takes alternate.
4. **The cap and truncation**, from the first round: the screenings whose retrieval was cut at the
   cap, those out of reach, those `truncated`, and those that would end `result_truncated`, with
   Wilson intervals; and `result_truncated` on the scale population at each preset.
5. **The re-screen** at 100, 300 and 1,000 changed entries and 200,000 subjects, three takes per
   size and tree, each take on its own freshly built database (`100k-import`, `rescreen-seed`,
   then the sizes in order). The time is the step's `rescreenMs`. `rescreen-check --size 1000` (the
   no-miss check) runs on the first database of each tree. **Run cost** is the runs inserted times
   the tree's 100,000-name screening p50 (the pooled p50 of its three measure takes); the **total
   cost** is the median re-screen time plus the run cost.
6. **The gate**, three times at the head with `OPENVERIFY_C787_TIMING=assert` (from
   `apps/api`: `vitest run --config vitest.integration.config.ts
   src/watchlists/watchlists.integration.test.ts`).
7. **The shares** of each switchable change of 6D: `unit-lengths`, `join-cache`, `name-lru`,
   `token-cache`, `indexed-matrix`, `ratio-check` and `token-pairs` (`SCREENING_SHORTCUTS`) and the
   read shortcut `array-parameters` (`useWatchlistReadShortcuts`). Each is measured at the head at
   both scales by `<scale>-shares --switch <name>`: in one call (one lock hold), a priming round,
   then three takes of a round with the switch off and a round with it on, alternately, every other
   switch on. Before each round the analysed-name and token caches are emptied and the 20 warm-ups
   run again. Every round's created matches are digested per subject and compared with the priming
   round's. A take's `worse` is its on round's p95 minus its off round's; the share is the median
   `worse` with its range. `matcher-6d` names the scale each targets (2,000,000 names for
   `name-lru`, `token-cache`, `indexed-matrix`, `ratio-check` and `token-pairs`); both are measured
   because the verdict reads both.
8. **Quality at the head** (C78.8), recall per variant source, precision and false alerts per
   1,000 for the 6B set and the scale population, rendered with decision 7's intervals (the list
   under test is fixed in every replicate). If the identity check passes, the base's numbers are
   the head's.

### The timing rule

6C's, unchanged:
- Every timing runs under `flock /tmp/openverify-e2e.lock`, one step per lock hold.
- **A timing counts only when the 1-minute load average is at most 4 at its start and at its end**
  (`/proc/loadavg`): a measure take reads the loads its round file records, a re-screen take the
  loads its record writes before and after the re-screen, a share take the start of its off round
  and the end of its on round, a gate take the loads around the call. Otherwise it is discarded and
  retaken when the machine is quieter.
- Before each timing the runner waits inside the lock for the 1-minute load to fall below 2.5, so
  the step's own load does not carry the end of a take over 4.
- **Three takes, the median**, with the load averages. The pooled p95 over the three rounds is
  printed beside the median.
- A take that never meets the load rule after repeated waits is reported with its loads and
  marked. No verdict rests on such a take alone.
- Counts (identity, runs, cap, truncation, listed found, unlisted alerts, quality) do not depend on
  the machine; they are read from the first take.

### Budgets (owner decision 1 after 6C, unchanged)

- C78.7 at 100,000 names: a median round p95 of at most **100 ms**.
- C78.7 at 2,000,000 names: at most **250 ms**.
- The re-screen at 1,000 changed entries and 200,000 subjects: a median of at most **60 s**.
- Small syncs: at 100 and at 300 changed entries, the head's total cost is no higher than the
  base's.

A budget is **met** or **missed**, with its gap. A budget still missed is carried under the stopping
rule of decision 1 after 6C, with its profile; it never moves.

### Decision rules

Fixed here, before the seed. **The margin is 5%** of the reference p95: the switch off for a share,
the base for a change that cannot be switched. Three takes cannot tell a smaller difference from
the noise of this machine (6D Stage 1's takes at one tree spread by 5 to 8%), and a change whose
created matches are identical is kept unless it measurably costs.

1. **The identity check: identical** when, for the 6B set and the scale population at both scales,
   `identity.json` reports `same`: every query on both sides, every (query, preset) pair digested,
   and every pair identical in the kept ids, `fixed`, `limits`, `errors` and digest. Otherwise
   **not identical**, with the differing pairs counted by field. The performance rounds' identity
   is reported beside it.
2. **The executed runs: kept** when, at both trees and each size, every inserted run was executed
   and kept a match on a changed entry (`pass`), and, at 1,000 entries, none is short of its full
   screening. The head's runs inserted and their subjects' digest are compared with the base's.
3. **Each switchable change: kept** when its share step's digests are identical (`identical: true`)
   at both scales and its median `worse` is at most 5% of its median off-round p95 at both scales.
   Otherwise **revert**, naming its commits.
4. **The changes that cannot be switched** (`matcher-6d`'s refactors: the analysed-name cache keyed
   without JSON, the unit refactor and the removal of `token-memo`; `rescreen-6d`'s borrowed
   speed-up of `keys/`): **kept** when the identity check is identical and the head's median round
   p95 is at most 5% above the base's at both scales; otherwise **revert**.
5. **`rescreen-6d`'s re-screen changes** (the frequencies read once, each pinned configuration
   resolved once, one screening per candidate subject per page, the name-bound check, the
   reverse-index postings, C004's page-wise run): **kept** when the executed runs are kept (rule 2),
   `rescreen-check` at the head finds no miss at 1,000 entries, the head inserts the same runs as
   the base at each size (same count and subjects' digest), and the head's median re-screen at
   1,000 entries is at most 5% above the base's; otherwise **revert**, naming the commits. Its
   frequency-table read has a test seam (`frequencyTableRows`), not a benchmark switch; its share
   is bounded by the `frequencies` phase the record states.
6. **The gate:** three takes at the head with the timing asserted. Its output must name the case
   `shipped-variant-groups` and pass its machine-independent checks; its timing assertion passes or
   fails, as reported, and its p95 is set beside the head's 100,000-name measurement.
7. **Each budget:** met or missed with its gap (above).

### What this measurement does not cover

- Real names, real lists, real customers and real data entry.
- Name frequencies other than the assumed Zipf law (exponent 1), and lists of other sizes.
- PEP positions, classes and relatives, adverse media, identifiers, vessels, aircraft, crypto
  addresses, weak aliases and the `custom` preset.
- A switch's share is measured with every other switch on, so shares that interact (`token-pairs`
  reads the tokens `token-cache` shares) are not additive.
- Re-screens of larger syncs, other environments' sizes, and the write cost of a sync.
- One machine; timings depend on it, and the load rule bounds how much.

## Amendments

Made after the method was committed. None changes a metric, a set, a rule or the margin, and no
benchmark code changed after the seed.

1. **Share retakes.** At 100,000 names the three-take calls of `name-lru` and `array-parameters`
   broke the load rule (1-minute loads up to 5.4 and 4.4 at a take's end, from processes outside
   the calibration). Each was retaken as three calls of one take (`--takes 1`), every take's off
   and on rounds still in one lock hold, after the load fell below 2.0. The discarded files are
   kept outside git. Every other share met the rule in its one call.
2. **Steps run side by side.** Steps that are not timings ran at both trees at once, in one lock
   hold: the 6B and 100,000-name quality steps, the 2,000,000-name scale screening, and parts 1 to
   4 of both 2,000,000-name imports (performance and scale). Parts 5 to 8 ran one tree at a time,
   so each call stayed under 10 minutes. The workers of `rescreen-check` and `rescreen-runs` ran 6
   side by side. No timing ran beside any of them.
3. **Load waits.** Inside the lock, the runner waited for the 1-minute load to fall below 2.5
   before each measure take and below 2.0 before each re-screen take, share and gate take.
4. **The identity steps' output.** Both identity steps write `identity.json` into their `--out`;
   the first performance identity was written into the quality one's directory and overwrote it.
   Both were run again into their own directories (`compare/quality`, `compare/performance`), with
   the same results. A follow-up gives the files distinct names.
5. **One run not in the method:** the head's 100,000-name performance steps again from an empty
   directory (see "Reproduction").

## Findings

Each is for its owner. None was acted on here.

1. **The C78.7 figures depend on the seed's draw of the table** (the owner, decision 1 after 6C).
   The tables' law is fixed, but which names take the top ranks is the seed's. On this seed the
   100,000-name table binds the cap in 25 of 300 searches (candidates p95 2,000), where the 6C
   evaluation seed's binds it in none (p95 630); the same head measures 180.9 ms here and 65.0 ms
   in the gate on the 6C seed's table. A budget judged on one draw is judged on that draw's head of
   names. The method of a later calibration could fix several seeds and report the spread, or
   judge the budget at a percentile over seeds. This also settles `platform-6d`'s question: the
   gate's machine-independent limit "the cap binds in at most 6 of 300" holds on the 6C seed's
   table, and this seed's table would break it at both trees, so it is a property of the
   published seed, not of the matcher.
2. **At 2,000,000 names the p95 is retrieval now** (Phase 11's performance hardening). 6D cut the
   scoring by two thirds at both scales; retrieval, which 6D left as 6C built it, is 594.8 of the
   head's 808.0 ms, in the searches that reach the cap or hold a pair of tokens out of reach (the
   pair lookup up to the cap, which keeps the listed namesakes). The next levers are there and in
   the list names' analysis (`matcher-6d`'s follow-up: store it at sync time).
3. **The re-screen misses 60 s by 14.3 s** (Phase 11). At 1,000 entries the screening of the
   candidate subjects takes 46 to 57 s and about 25 s does not depend on the sync's size (the
   subjects' reading and decryption, their specs, the reverse index's keys and build).
   `rescreen-6d`'s follow-ups name the levers: a token-pair memo for the name-bound check, worker
   threads per chunk, a prepared index kept between re-screens.
4. **`result_truncated` above the 1% target on this draw** (the owner, decision 1 after 6B). At
   `tolerant` it ends 2.55% of the scale population's person screenings at 100,000 names and 1.80%
   at 2,000,000, identically at both trees. The search is cut at the cap with nothing kept; it is
   reported as truncated, never as no match (C78.5). 6C's draw gave 0.55%.
5. **False alerts on common names stay high** (as 6C's finding 4): at `tolerant`, 16.9% of person
   customers alert against 100,000 names and 47.8% against 2,000,000; at 2,000,000 names a listed
